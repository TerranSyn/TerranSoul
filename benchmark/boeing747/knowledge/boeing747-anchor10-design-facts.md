# Boeing 747 — Anchor-10 Design Facts (real-world reference material)

Human-owned public design knowledge about the real Boeing 747 aircraft, organized by the
visual feature a modeler judges. This is reference material about how the real jet is shaped,
NOT a rubric answer key and NOT the geometry of any specific 3D file. Every fact below is a
published aircraft-design characteristic (Boeing 747-400 class), the kind an engineer or
aviation reference book states about the real airplane.

## engines_four_underwing — the four engines
The 747 has FOUR high-bypass turbofan engines (Pratt & Whitney PW4000 / GE CF6 / Rolls-Royce
RB211 class). Each engine hangs from a pylon (strut) that mounts it BELOW the wing and clearly
FORWARD of the wing leading edge — the entire nacelle inlet sits ahead of the wing, not tucked
underneath it. A nacelle is a large-diameter fan cowl roughly 4.5 m long with a fan about
2.5–2.9 m in diameter. The inlet has a rounded lip, and the inlet face is an OPEN dark ring
because the fan is visible inside — so the front of the engine reads darker than the cowl body.
Spanwise placement: the inboard engine sits at roughly one-third of the wing semi-span, the
outboard at roughly two-thirds of the semi-span. The inboard engine is mounted slightly more
forward and slightly lower than the outboard engine. Key reads that separate a convincing 747
engine from a plain cylinder: (1) the whole inlet is ahead of the wing leading edge, (2) the
inlet face is open/darker than the cowl, (3) four of them, correctly spaced, each on a pylon.

## upper_deck_hump — the upper deck
The upper deck is a SECOND, SMALLER-radius lobe faired onto the top of the main fuselage tube —
a double-bubble cross-section, not a box or block sitting on top. It begins AT the cockpit at
the extreme nose (the flight deck is on the front face of the hump), is widest near the front,
and fairs DOWN into the main crown through a gentle aft ramp / S-curve that ends around the front
third of the fuselage length. There is NO vertical step or seam where the hump meets the main
crown — the two skins blend continuously. A short separate row of upper-deck windows runs along
the side of the hump. The single most important read: the hump is a smooth blended lobe with a
tapering aft fairing, never a discrete raised block.

## fuselage_proportions — the body
The 747 fuselage is about 70 m long and about 6.5 m in diameter, giving a slender ~10.8:1
fineness ratio — one long continuous tube, not a stubby barrel. Two ends define it: (1) a long
OGIVE NOSE — a streamlined tapering radome that curves smoothly to a rounded point, NOT a flat
end cap and NOT a hemisphere; (2) an UPSWEPT TAIL — the aft fuselage sweeps upward into a long
tapering tail cone (this upsweep gives ground clearance for rotation on takeoff), with the APU
exhaust at the very tip of the cone. The skin is continuous with no visible segment seams
between nose, barrel, and tail. A flat-ended cylinder is the classic failure; the real body is
pointed-and-tapered at both ends.

## window_door_lines — windows and doors
The main-deck passenger windows form a SINGLE continuous, evenly-spaced row of small windows at
mid-fuselage height, running most of the body length. That row is BROKEN at intervals by taller
passenger DOORS — the 747 main deck has about five door pairs. The upper deck carries its own
separate, shorter window row on the hump. A livery cheatline (a painted stripe) typically runs
along the main window line. The details that read as authentic: the door breaks interrupting the
window rhythm, and the distinct second (upper-deck) row.

## silhouette_747 — overall recognizable shape
The instantly-recognizable 747 read is the sum of: a long slender fuselage with a pointed ogive
nose and upswept tail; the forward upper-deck hump blended into the crown; swept-back wings; four
podded engines slung forward and below the wings; and a tall swept vertical fin. When the hump is
faired (no step), the nose and tail taper are present, and the engines sit forward, the silhouette
becomes unmistakably 747 — the shape is composed of those same feature fixes, not a separate thing
to tune.

## craftsmanship — clean joins, no seams
A convincing model has every junction faired: the hump-to-crown blend, the wing-root-to-belly
fairing, and the nacelle-to-pylon-to-wing attachment are all smooth continuous joins with no
visible steps, gaps, or floating parts. Craftsmanship rises automatically as the discrete-step
hump, the flat fuselage caps, and the floating engine cylinders are replaced by blended, tapered,
pylon-mounted forms.

## supporting geometry (already reads well — preserve, do not regress)
Wing: leading-edge / quarter-chord sweep about 37.5°; span about 64 m (roughly 0.9× the fuselage
length); about 7° dihedral; strong root-to-tip taper; wing roots low-mounted and faired into a
belly fairing. Tail: a tall swept vertical fin plus a low, swept horizontal tailplane. Landing
gear: the signature five-group arrangement — a two-wheel nose gear plus two four-wheel wing bogies
plus two four-wheel body (fuselage) bogies, eighteen wheels total.
