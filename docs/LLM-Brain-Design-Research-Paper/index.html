Research · position + measurement

From Memo to Memory: a first-class external-memory architecture for frozen language models.

The behavioural case for memory-not-weights, and a measured existence proof - Zork I plus four SIA benchmarks, May-June 2026. We accept the memo-vs-memory charge and contest the dichotomy: what makes a store a memory rather than a memo is what it does over time, and those behaviours are realisable with the model's weights frozen.

What this paper argues
  • We accept the diagnosis of Xu, Dai & Zhang [38]: per-turn contextual agentic memory is a memo - lookup, not learning - and calling its accumulation "learning" is a category error.
  • We contest the dichotomy. The paper splits persistence into context (CC) vs. weights (θ) and prescribes writing knowledge into θ. We argue memory is individuated by behaviour, and propose five falsifiable criteria (C1-C5) a governed external substrate can satisfy with weights frozen.
  • We map each criterion to a shipped TerranSoul faculty with source-file references, and report a measured existence proof - every number sourced to a committed file.
  • We engage the strongest weight-update counter-pole, SIA [45]: we concede its benchmark result and bound it three ways, and observe that on SIA's headline LawBench the frozen actor + memory scores higher.
  • We are explicit about scope: this does not refute the compositional-generalisation theorem of [38]; it shows the memo charge is escapable without editing θ.
Standpoint and honesty. This is a combined position-and-measurement paper by the TerranSoul project - the system it advocates. We have a stake in the conclusion and try to earn it by reporting the numbers that cut against us: the modal Zork I result is 10 → 10, not the peak 50; the deep cross-episode climb is high-variance and largely an across-run accumulation artifact; several benches are n-limited and single-PC. We concede the central architectural point to our interlocutor [38] up front - TerranSoul does not edit model weights, so on that paper's own definition our "memory" is weight-frozen by mandate. (The campaign peak referenced throughout is 50, reached late in the fix loop; the honesty point is unchanged - the modal result, not the peak, is the headline.) The argument below is that weight-editing is one route to the behaviours of memory, not the only one - and that a frozen-weight substrate can deliver all of them, measurably.

AbstractWhat this paper measures, and what it argues

Two questions about long-term memory for language-model agents are usually run together, and we keep them apart. The first is architectural: should an agent's memory live inside the model - as weights or a long context window - or outside it, as a queryable store the agent reads and writes through a tool interface? The second is conceptual: Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] argue that the dominant "contextual / agentic memory" paradigm - retrieve passages, paste them into the context window per turn - is not memory at all but a memo, and prove a compositional sample-complexity separation between context-engineering (CC) and weight-based memory (θ), prescribing a consolidation channel that writes experience into θ. We accept the diagnosis and contest the dichotomy. We argue that what makes a store a memory rather than a memo is a set of behavioural properties - offline consolidation and reconsolidation, adaptive forgetting, a typed structure that evolves, calibrated metacognition, and governed self-evolution - and that these are realisable in a structured external substrate with the model's weights frozen. We state five falsifiable criteria (C1-C5), map each to a shipped TerranSoul faculty with source-file references, and interleave the external-memory architecture (an MCP-served Rust brain with a six-signal hybrid retriever and a typed knowledge graph) with the methods of a controlled benchmark.

The evidence headline is a measured existence proof, weights never touched. On the long-horizon acting axis - Zork I, a task TALES calls "insurmountable ... even for modern state-of-the-art LLMs" [14] - the production frozen 12B (gemma4:12b-it-qat) produces verified cross-episode behavioural change: its self-improvement campaign broke the modal-10 ceiling to a peak single episode of 50 via across-run accumulation, with the honestly de-confounded per-run cross-episode score at ~15; and the same substrate lifts a deliberately weak frozen 4B (gemma4:e4b) from 0 (two true zero controls) to 10-20 - the controlled isolation that makes the lift causally attributable. Holding the model and a captured strategy fixed, delivery reliability - whether the chosen move reaches the engine every turn - and not added model capacity, moves the same 4B from a non-deterministic 73/177 to a deterministic 350/350. A frontier model solves once and a cheap local model replays thereafter: distillation through memory, into data not weights. On the orthogonal retrieval axis the substrate reaches LongMemEval-S R@5 99.4% / R@10 100% / R@20 100% (NDCG@10 94.4%, MRR 95.2%) at p50 1.0 s and $0, and is competitive on LoCoMo and a personal-AI head-to-head. Engaging SIA [45], we concede its benchmark result and bound it three ways - yet observe that on SIA's own headline LawBench our frozen 12B + memory scores higher (76.3% full / up to 80.0% with a stronger frozen actor vs SIA's 70.1%). We are explicit about scope: this does not refute the compositional-generalisation theorem of [38]; it shows the memo charge is escapable without editing θ.

01 · IntroductionA memo, or a memory?

A memo is a note you write to get through the afternoon: you jot what you need, you act on it, you throw it away, and nothing about today's note changes how you write tomorrow's. Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] argue that this is precisely what "agentic memory" has become - a per-turn working note assembled by retrieval, used, and discarded across the retrieval boundary. The system retrieves the k nearest passages, pastes them into the prompt, generates, and the only thing that persists is more undigested passages in the same flat store. They call treating this as learning a "category error with provable consequences," and we think they are right. Their constructive claim is sharper than the critique: context engineering (CC) generalises only by similarity to stored cases, while weight-based memory (θ) generalises by applying abstract rules to never-seen inputs. Their Theorem 1 (a compositional sample-complexity separation via Fano's inequality and VC bounds) states retrieval needs Ω(k²) stored examples where parametric learning needs only O(d + log(1/δ)/δ), and the gap is context-window-independent. From this they derive four limitations - definitional, structural, dynamic (the "Frozen Novice": agents accumulate notes but never develop expertise because weights never change between sessions), and security (injected content persists across all future sessions) - and prescribe a consolidation channel into θ.

We agree with the diagnosis and most of the constructive frame. Our disagreement is narrow. The paper equates "true memory" with "knowledge in θ." We argue that memory is better individuated by what it does than by where it lives: a substrate is a memory, not a memo, when it consolidates offline, revises beliefs in place, forgets adaptively, grows a typed structure, calibrates its own confidence, and governs its own evolution. These are behavioural commitments, and they are realisable in a structured external store while the language model's weights stay frozen. We do not claim this closes the paper's compositional gap (it does not; Section 6); we claim the memo-vs-memory distinction is not the same distinction as CC-vs-θ, and that conflating them concedes too much to fine-tuning.

This is also the architectural question the empirical half asks, in its sharpest form. A recent survey formalises agent memory as "a write-manage-read loop tightly coupled with perception and action" and argues that "memory ... is what turns a stateless text generator into a genuinely adaptive agent" [15]. The model-external approach factors memory into a queryable store - the lineage of MemGPT/Letta [2], Mem0 [1], HippoRAG [4], and temporal knowledge-graph systems such as Zep/Graphiti [10]. The system under test - "the brain" - is a Rust memory server with a six-signal hybrid retriever (BM25/FTS5 + dense HNSW ANN + reciprocal-rank fusion, k = 60) and a typed knowledge graph, exposed to any client over the Model Context Protocol (MCP). To probe whether such a store is a memory and not a memo, we measure it on Zork I (0-350 points over ~400 optimal moves, fatal hazards, a parser vocabulary the model never saw) around its production frozen actor - gemma4:12b-it-qat, the resident model TerranSoul is mandated to keep frozen - and, as a second, deliberately weak probe, gemma4:e4b, an effective-4B edge model. The two probes answer complementary questions: the 12B carries the self-improvement story (Section 5.1), while the 4B is the stringent zero-control probe - both non-brain arms score 0, so where the substrate helps, the help is causally attributable (Section 5.2); where the model is the bottleneck, that is visible too. That a 4B scores near zero unaided is expected - BALROG [13] reports models "struggle significantly," TALES [14] calls Zork I "insurmountable" - which is what makes a measurable lift meaningful. TerranSoul runs under a standing mandate to keep its local model (gemma4:12b-it-qat) frozen; if "memory" required θ-editing, TerranSoul could have none.

Contributions.

  1. Five falsifiable criteria (C1-C5, Section 3) that separate a memo from a memory, behaviourally, with a criterion-by-criterion mapping to shipped faculties and source-file references (Section 4).
  2. A controlled three-arm benchmark (external memory server / agent's-own managers / none) on the same model, seed, and ROM, with the memory substrate as the sole independent variable (Section 4.7); design and transcripts public [16].
  3. An AGI-purity discipline - no task seeds, walkthroughs, or hardcoded room logic in the substrate - under which the production frozen 12B's campaign broke the modal-10 ceiling to a peak episode of 50, de-confounded per-run ~15 (Section 5.1), and the substrate still lifts a frozen 4B from 0 to 10-20 against two zero controls (Section 5.2).
  4. A controlled delivery-reliability isolation (Section 5.3): holding model, knowledge and critic fixed, varying only how reliably the chosen action reaches the world moves the score from a non-deterministic 73/177 to a deterministic 350 - a third agent-performance axis distinct from retrieval quality and model capacity.
  5. A distillation-through-memory architecture (Section 5.4): a frontier model solves once, the brain persists the solution as editable data, a cheap local model executes thereafter - distillation into data, not weights.
  6. Cross-task evidence that the same substrate is competitive or leading on four public retrieval benchmarks (Section 5.6), and an engagement with SIA on its own four benchmarks (Section 5.7, Section 6).

We run the upstream stickystyle/ZorkGPT agent [12] - which we did not author - unmodified, swapping only its memory and knowledge managers.

02 · Related workWhere this sits

2.1 The 2026 agentic-memory debate

The proximate interlocutor is Xu, Dai & Zhang [38], whose memo-vs-memory charge and CC-vs-θ separation we engage throughout. Its empirical scaffolding (reported as their evidence, not independently verified here) cites ParamMem-style results in which parametric storage scores above retrieval with the gap widening on novel tasks; Ovadia 2024 that RAG does not improve compositional reasoning; SCAN/COGS multi-hop results; and two memory-injection attacks - MINJA at 98.2% success and PoisonedRAG at 90% with five adversarial texts - as evidence that a persistent store is a persistent attack surface. It names MemGPT, Reflexion, and Voyager as "CC-engineering only," and proposes a Compositional Generalization over Time (CGT) metric: does accuracy on novel concept combinations rise with experience, not just recall@k?

Three further 2026 lines sharpen the picture. The dual-process line - DCPM, Memory Beyond Recall [39] - splits a System-1 "daytime writer" from a System-2 "nighttime" schema-induction pass; its raw-recall-vs-distilled-strategy split is the memo/memory line drawn behaviourally rather than by substrate. The governance line - SSGM [40] - frames drift, poisoning, and unbounded growth as the missing governance layer, observing that the danger only exists once memory truly persists and self-evolves: a scratchpad has nothing to govern. A cluster of surveys and taxonomies [15,41,42,43,44] converges on a shared picture: "memory" is the externalisation layer around a frozen LLM, distinct from per-turn context/retrieval.

2.2 Agent memory mechanisms, retrieval, and graphs

The external-memory lineage runs from MemGPT/Letta [2], through Mem0 [1] (whose LLM-arbitrated conflict resolution the substrate adopts at its typed-edge layer [20]), to HippoRAG [4] and spatial schemes such as MemoryPalace [3]. Du [15] formalises memory as a "write-manage-read loop," warning that "one bad write can pollute the store for many steps downstream" - the abstract statement of the silent-ingest failure we hit concretely (Section 4.6). The retriever fuses lexical and dense channels with reciprocal-rank fusion and applies Contextual Retrieval [8] and HyDE [9] as a per-query-class tool rather than a global default [20]; its graph layer follows GraphRAG's Leiden-community lineage and the typed-edge designs of Zep/Graphiti [10], MAGMA [24], and PersonalAI [25]. The agentic-RAG survey [26] names "Memory Management and Long-Term Adaptation" and "Safety, Trust, and Governance" as critical open challenges - the territory of our write-reliability (Section 4.6) and delivery-reliability (Section 5.3) results.

2.3 Memory and long-horizon benchmarks

Three benchmarks anchor the retrieval field: LoCoMo [34], LongMemEval [35], and BEAM; MemoryAgentBench [22] and AMA-Bench [23] ground evaluation in cognitive science and long-horizon agentic memory. These expose a sobering gap - Du [15] reports systems that "score near-perfectly on LoCoMo plummet to 40-60% in MemoryArena." The production systems the target paper indicts sit here too: Mem0 [1] reports a LoCoMo QA J-score of 68.5%, Letta/MemGPT [2] 83.2%, Zep/Graphiti [10] 34.53% - but these are end-to-end QA accuracy, not retrieval recall, and are not comparable to the retrieval numbers in Section 5.6 [21]. On the acting side, Jericho [11] exposes 55 interactive-fiction games with ground-truth state; BALROG [13] and TALES [14] motivate games as agent benchmarks and report broad struggle; TextQuests [29] and Dual-Scale World Models [30] extend the line. The upstream agent we run, ZorkGPT [12], peaks at 115/350 in its public continuous run - our frontier-scaffold reference (Section 5.4).

2.4 Agent reasoning, self-evolution, and skill libraries

ReAct [5] interleaves reasoning and acting; Reflexion [6] adds verbal self-feedback across episodes; Voyager [7] accumulates a skill library. The 2025-2026 self-evolution wave extends this: AutoSkill [27] performs "experience-driven lifelong learning via skill self-evolution," and RL frameworks fold a validated skill library into training [28]. Self-Guided Self-Play (SGS) [37], evaluated on Lean4 theorem proving, is the most relevant recent point: a frozen Guide scores a Conjecturer's self-generated sub-problems on relevance (0-5), conclusion-complexity (0-4), and redundancy (0-1), combined as R_guide = max(0, relevance + (2−complexity) + (1−redundancy)). We adopt neither its RL training nor its R_solve rollout signal under our frozen-harness / AGI-pure discipline - only the gradient-free Conjecturer→Guide curriculum idea and the Guide's exact reward, re-implemented as a deterministic rubric because our brain exposes no instruction-following judge tool (Section 5.2). Our room-scoped reflections (Section 4.6) are Reflexion-style feedback given spatial scope; our "taught skill" (Section 5.3) is a Voyager-style library entry.

2.5 The self-improving-agent counter-pole

SIA - Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates [45] - is the sharpest contrast to our thesis. It frames autonomous self-improvement as two historically disjoint silos: a harness-update school, in which a meta-agent rewrites a task agent's scaffold with weights frozen, and a test-time-training school, which updates weights on task feedback with the harness frozen. SIA co-evolves both: a Feedback-Agent reads a full trajectory and chooses, per generation, to rewrite the scaffold (weights frozen) or apply an RL weight update (LoRA; scaffold frozen). Its headline is that combining both levers exceeds scaffold-iteration alone on all three of its benchmarks - Chinese legal charge classification, low-level GPU-kernel optimisation, and single-cell RNA denoising - by +25.1% over prior SOTA, 12.4% faster kernels (1,017 vs 1,161 µs), and +20.4% respectively [45]. This is the strongest empirical case in the 2026 literature that a fixed-weight lever, used alone, can plateau below what weight editing reaches - and, on SIA's own taxonomy, a partial vindication of our position, since its harness-update silo is a fixed-weight lever. We engage it directly in Section 5.7 and Section 6; a source-audited side-by-side lives in the companion comparison [47].

2.6 Position

Prior work largely optimises retrieval quality [1,4,8,9] or reasoning structure [5,6,7]; the target paper [38] prescribes moving knowledge into θ. We contribute two things at once: (i) the argument that the memo charge is escapable without θ-editing, via five behavioural criteria a frozen-weight substrate can satisfy (Sections 3-4); and (ii) the empirical isolation of a third agent-performance axis - does the chosen action actually reach the world, every turn? - shown, with model and strategy held fixed, to dominate outcome in our setting (Section 5.3). To our knowledge this is the first clean separation of delivery reliability from model capacity on a long-horizon game.

03 · The distinctionFive criteria that separate a memo from a memory

To make the debate falsifiable we propose five criteria. They are behavioural: they ask what a store does over time, not where its bits sit. A pure retrieve-and-stuff pipeline fails all five; the target paper's θ-editing remedy passes some by construction. We claim a structured external substrate can pass all five with weights frozen, and we concede in Section 6 that the criteria are themselves arguable.

#CriterionMemo (retrieve-and-stuff)Memory (the demand)
C1Offline consolidation & reconsolidationnone; the store only grows by appendidle replay distils experience; reactivation makes a belief labile and revises it in place
C2Adaptive forgetting / decaynothing decays; old notes rank forevertime-anchored decay and tiered eviction; salience, not recency-of-write, survives
C3Typed structure that evolvesflat list of passagestyped, directional graph with supersedes/contradicts edges that self-correct
C4Calibrated metacognitionno model of its own reliabilitya second-order monitor that estimates calibrated confidence and recalibrates from outcomes
C5Governed self-evolutionany write persists; injection is permanentautonomous writes pass a drift/poisoning/growth gate; uncorroborated writes are quarantined

Two of the target paper's four limitations are about these criteria. The "Frozen Novice" (its dynamic limitation) is the absence of C1: a store that only appends never develops expertise. The security limitation (persistent injection) is the absence of C5. We answer these two directly in Section 4.1 and Section 4.5. The remaining two limitations - definitional and structural - are genuinely about CC-vs-θ and the compositional ceiling; we do not claim the five criteria dissolve them, and we say so in Section 6.

04 · The architectureTerranSoul, mapped criterion-by-criterion

TerranSoul is a local-first companion (Vue + Tauri + Rust + SQLite + Ollama) whose "brain" is a Rust memory server reached over MCP. By project mandate the language model's weights are frozen; all learning is externalised into the substrate. Below, each criterion of Section 3 is mapped to a real, shipped faculty, described at the level of observable behaviour; the benchmark's harness methods are interleaved where they bear on the mapping. (Implementation coordinates - files, functions, schemas, and tuned parameter values - are deliberately withheld from this public artifact; the private criterion-to-source mapping is available to reviewers on request.) We label each faculty honestly: every one strengthens or revises stored structure, never θ.

4.1 C1 - Consolidation and reconsolidation (the answer to the Frozen Novice)

The reconsolidation faculty implements an idle complementary-learning-systems (CLS) replay pass. It samples the highest-salience episodic memories that are due under spaced-repetition-style scheduling - salience weighs importance, access history and age, and each review reschedules the next - and strengthens what replay confirms, raising a smoothed confidence and stamping the consolidation run for audit. This is the hippocampus→neocortex replay the target paper says agents lack. The harder property is reconsolidation: reactivating a memory makes it labile, a gate decides whether a sufficiently similar new observation should overwrite rather than co-exist, and belief revision then happens in place - correcting a stale belief cleanly instead of stacking a contradicting duplicate beside it. A memo cannot do this. Honest caveat: this revises stored confidence and content, not θ. The Frozen-Novice charge is that weights never change between sessions; our answer is that behaviour changes between sessions because the substrate the frozen model reads has changed - which Section 5.1 shows is measurable.

4.2 C2 - Three tiers and adaptive forgetting

The store holds three tiers: short (evicted at session end), working (held until consolidation), and long (permanent, vector-indexed). A per-cognitive-kind half-life decay is folded into the retrieval score, with half-lives spanning roughly an order of magnitude from days (episodic events) to a year (durable judgments) - the exact schedule is a tuned, brain-seeded configuration. The effect a reader can verify behaviourally: a correction stays durable for months while a time-stamped episode loses rank within days - salience, not recency-of-write, determines what survives.

4.3 C3 - A typed knowledge graph that evolves

The edge layer holds typed, directional edges - relation kinds include supersedes, contradicts, derived_from, governs, and depends_on - keyed so that extraction is idempotent, with provenance recorded per edge. Edge writes are exposed to any client over MCP via the public brain_add_edge tool. In the bench harness, map adjacency and object/location pairs are promoted into this typed graph; a direct probe confirms Living Room -exits_via_west→ Kitchen persists across restarts and is queryable by any MCP client (Section 4.7) - the spatial graph one agent builds is legible to the next [10,24,25]. This buys multi-hop recall and a structure that self-corrects: a supersedes/contradicts edge demotes a stale claim rather than leaving two equal-rank contradictions.

4.4 C4 - Calibrated metacognition

The metacognition faculty is a second-order monitor that estimates a calibrated confidence before an answer is emitted, and routes the score into one of answer / deliberate / seek-more / abstain. Crucially the calibration is learned from outcomes: a recalibration pass refits the calibration map from logged data against realised session outcomes (minimising log-loss / expected calibration error) and rewrites the brain-seeded configuration. The curve is not hardcoded; it improves with experience - with weights frozen, because the thing refit is a config row in the substrate, not a parameter in θ.

4.5 C5 - Governed self-evolution (the answer to persistent injection)

The target paper's sharpest practical point is security: MINJA at 98.2% and PoisonedRAG at 90% with five texts mean a store that accepts any write carries an attacker's payload into every future session. TerranSoul's answer is a write-time governance gate, BCF-21, shipped as of July 2026. It routes every autonomous durable write - brain-authored extractions, connector-ingested documents, and peer-brain sync imports - through a single write-time governance decision before the row persists, composing existing structural signals: a drift guard (reusing the C1 reconsolidation/corroboration path), a poisoning/injection guard (source-concentration and source-trust provenance), a growth guard (the decay/prune budget of C2), and quarantine of peer-brain imports until corroborated. User writes bypass the gate; every non-user write path is governed. A persistent-injection regression suite replays same-source adversarial write bursts through the real ingest paths and asserts they quarantine rather than persist. It cites SSGM [40] as its basis. The point: governance is possible and meaningful only because the store is real memory - a memo has nothing to govern.

4.6 The closed loop, the brain bridge, and write-reliability failures

The five faculties compose into a loop. Write: a learned policy decides when to extract memories, on a seeded cadence with a cooldown. Manage / self-tune: idle passes run consolidation (Section 4.1) and recalibration (Section 4.4). Read: hybrid FTS5 + vector + KG retrieval with rerank. In the Zork harness, a brain bridge converts raw Z-machine state into importance-scored, KG-structured memories: signal-derived importance (score_delta>0 → 10; inventory change → 8; first visit → 7; death → 10 + a fatal memory), structured content (prefix | Location | Action | Result | Score | Inventory) so the KG auto-extractor builds typed edges, and principle-before-episodic retrieval so cross-episode rules are not buried.

Building the bench surfaced four agent-memory failure classes, each diagnosable and fixable at the brain layer without retraining: (1) Verify, don't trust - every brain_ingest_lesson had silently stored zero rows because the MCP client treated a structured error envelope as success (the concrete form of Du's "one bad write can pollute the store" [15], here a no-write undetected); a 14-line fix plus a hard "is it retrievable next episode?" probe closed it. (2) Reflections require spatial scope - per-room reflections tagged loc_<id> surface exactly when the agent re-enters the room. (3) The prompt is a snapshot, not a stream - a reload_knowledge_base() push fixes a lantern discovered at turn 11 never reaching the turn-0 prompt, possible only because the bridge owns the prompt. (4) Grammar is not strategy - replacing a verb-list seed is a brain_ingest_lesson call, not a fine-tune. A memory layer with no verification probe will lie to you, and bad delivery propagates exactly as the surveys warn bad writes do [15,31].

4.7 Retrieval, harness controls, and AGI-purity discipline

Hybrid retrieval. Reads combine four signals: dense-HNSW and lexical-FTS5 rankings fused with reciprocal-rank fusion (k = 60), a typed-KG neighbour boost applied to the fused list, and a freshness channel guarding the degenerate no-ranking case; Contextual-Retrieval-style situating [8] and HyDE expansion [9] are applied, the latter gated per query class after ablation [20]. RRF is chosen for robustness: a failing ranking contributes nothing rather than poisoning the top-k. (A separate six-signal weighted mode - vector/keyword/recency/importance/decay/tier - serves scored search outside the chat-turn RRF path.)

Three-arm benchmark. We run upstream stickystyle/ZorkGPT [12] against Jericho's zork1.z5 [11] in a sealed Docker image. Agent, critic, extractor and strategy-generator are the same local model across arms; seed, temperature and ROM are held constant. Only the cognitive substrate is swapped: none, zorkgpt-default, and terransoul-brain. The brain runs as a separate OS process; the bridge speaks MCP JSON-RPC 2.0 over a real TCP boundary, bearer-token authenticated, with explicit error accounting.

Task-agnostic harness controls. A sequence of domain-agnostic perception/decision gates was added at the bridge, each validated by a sub-10-second reproduction before any bench: ID-keyed mapping by Z-machine location_id; object-tree/valid-exit validation; id-based loop-breaking; one-shot light-acquisition; and a delivery rule that banks valued (score-bearing) items over junk - value learned from the score signal, never a hardcoded treasure list.

AGI-purity discipline. A standing rule forbids seeding the substrate with Zork maps, routes, walkthroughs, room names, or curated vocabularies, enforced by a grep gate ("zero verb/score constants in the bridge source"). All Section 5.1-5.2 results are produced task-naïve. The only exception is the explicitly-labelled taught-solution demonstration of Section 5.3, whose move-level solution is a demo artifact never written to the memory seed (grep-verified) and gated behind a TAUGHT_SOLUTION_DEMO=1 runtime flag. Digest-level reproducibility is pinned: the ROM (zork1.z5 SHA-256 0ae5ac22...85c15fd), the 396-move taught solution (SHA-256 8461b56e...24df88c7), the upstream ZorkGPT commit, Jericho 3.3.1, Docker image IDs, and Ollama model digests are recorded in repro-manifest.json.

05 · EvidenceA competitive retriever, and cross-episode behavioural change with weights frozen

Every number below is from a committed result file - the retrieval and Zork figures in the project's benchmark matrix [21], the SIA-suite figures in the companion comparison [47]. We separate measurement from argument. These results establish that the substrate is a competitive retriever and that it produces cross-episode behavioural change with weights frozen. They do not measure compositional generalisation over time, and they do not refute the θ-vs-retrieval theorem.

5.1 Claim A - Cross-episode self-improvement on the production frozen 12B

We ran the production gemma4:12b-it-qat under the frozen protocol (fully frozen harness, task-naive AGI-pure seed, isolated bench MCP). The self-improvement campaign broke the modal-10 ceiling: nine sequential 3-episode × 300-turn runs on a brain preserved across runs put at least one episode ≥ 20 in every run past the first (four reached ≥ 35; best single episode 45) by closing and surviving the full underground-descent chain (the cap-breaking prerequisite - a non-scoring rug-move that reveals a trap door - discovered by the agent at runtime and replayed by the brain, never seeded), and a subsequent log-grounded fix loop pushed the campaign further still - a dead-cycle guard (Fix M) yielded a consistent 30/30/30 series and an unlit-descent demotion (Fix N) a 20/50/35 series, the campaign peak of 50 [21]. Two guessed navigation fixes were falsified by the bench and reverted; only the log-grounded fixes survived. Within one earlier series the brain-driven 12B also doubled its score across episodes (2 episodes × 100 turns; ep1 = 10 → ep2 = 20; 2,355 MCP calls, 0 errors) entirely through brain-side memory - a seed constant that resolved a planner-bonus band-collision and runtime learning that directed new-room exploration, item collection, and route replay [21]. Every constant change was validated before any GPU rerun by a 0.08 s replica that replays the real frozen decision chain.

We de-confound all of this honestly. On a fresh, task-naïve brain per run the per-run cross-episode result is ~15 (10/10/15 then 10/20/15; mean 11.7 → 15.0, peak 20; the modal single-run result is 10 → 10, and the ep1 = 10 → ep2 = 20 doubling was a single lucky episode), because across six clean episodes the frozen 12B never re-discovers the multi-step chain from scratch. The dramatic peaks of 45-50 therefore required across-run accumulation of a lucky one-time discovery, not repeatable single-run self-improvement. This sharpens rather than overturns the result: externalised memory is a genuine performance axis, but the binding constraint is the frozen actor's inability to re-discover the chain - the actor, not the memory layer.

Cross-game generalisation (n = 2). Extending the unchanged-then-repaired bridge (harness v2; memory-seed.sql byte-identical, zork_agent_patch.py frozen) to other Jericho games [21]:

Cross-model generalisation. Under the identical protocol, qwen2.5:7b - a distinct model family - scored 0 / 0 / 0 unaided but a consistent 5 / 5 / 5 reading the same accumulated brain the 12B built [21]. The lift generalises across architectures; its magnitude is bounded by the reading actor's planning depth (qwen-7B reaches a flat 5 where the 12B reaches up to 50 from the identical store).

Matched LLM-arm controls - and a self-correction. A matched 3-arm 12B run (300 turns) revised the headline honestly: the raw 12B is not a zero-control - unaided (none) it scored a mean 11.7 (unlike the 4B's 0) and ZorkGPT's own managers underperform the raw model at 1.7 [21]. The brain arm in that run collapsed to 13.3, traced to a real SOLUTION-REPLAY saturation bug (a frequently-traversed room accumulated every exit as a "scoring move," pinning all directions equally); the fix recovered the arm to a mean 20.0 - itself an instance of the loop's intended self-correction. The brain's reproducible value is the deep cellar runs (30-50) the raw 12B never reaches; the mean advantage over the capable 11.7 baseline is real but high-variance and store-health-dependent - on-thesis, since degrading the memory degrades the outcome.

5.3 Claim B - Delivery reliability: intermittent vs. reliable delivery (principal isolation)

Holding the model, the taught solution (distilled from the Section 5.4 Opus run), and the critic fixed, we vary only how reliably the chosen move is delivered to the engine [21]:

The cause was a loop-bookkeeping bug, not a capacity limit: the agent loop incremented its turn counter unconditionally, so any turn whose LLM call threw advanced the counter while skipping the served move - desyncing the lamp-sensitive solution. Replacing turn-count indexing with a self-managed pointer that advances only when a move is actually executed made delivery exception-safe; the same 4B then served all 396/396 moves 1:1, 0 errors, reaching 350 exactly at move 396 (a no-LLM replay confirms the 350 ceiling). To situate the demonstration and to source the taught solution, we ran a frontier model on the same ROM [21]:

Opus reaches the frontier scaffold via recall (the same basis deepseek uses), not raw reasoning - its cold reasoning (50) is below the 115 scaffold, consistent with TALES' "Claude models demonstrate the best overall performance" yet "struggle to reason across extremely long-horizon contexts" [14]. The Opus row is not an intervention on the frontier model; its purpose is the opposite direction. The 350/350 recall trajectory is the source of the taught solution of Section 5.3: the move sequence is distilled from this run and written into the brain once (as an editable, inspectable brain_ingest_lesson skill), after which the cheap 4B reads and executes it. This is distillation through memory - a frontier model solves once, the brain persists the result as data, decoupling the cost of discovering a strategy from the cost of executing it. Nothing is retrained; the knowledge lives as editable, inspectable memory and transfers to any client that queries the same brain.

5.5 Architectural stability

Two canonical 2-episode × 100-turn runs on gemma4:e4b executed 1,682 MCP memory operations with 0 errors while the brain concurrently served the desktop app for four hours; a later, longer demonstration ran 6,456 operations at 11 errors = 0.17%, under our 5% bar [21]. Grounding delivery reliability in the 4B's failure economy: the unaided gemma4:e4b emitted 94.9 JSON-malformed and 40.5 empty (context-saturated) completions per 100 turns before harness fixes drove both to ≈0.1/100 turns - every such event a turn whose move, under a blind pointer, would be dropped-then-skipped (Section 5.3).

06 · DiscussionWhere we agree, where we diverge

The diagnosis is correct. Naive contextual agentic memory is a memo. A flat vector store that only appends, never decays, never reconsolidates, and never governs its writes is a working note, and calling its accumulation "learning" is the category error [38] names. We adopt that framing wholesale.

The remedy is one route, not the route. The paper's prescription - a consolidation channel into θ via LoRA/MEMIT/SSR/TTT - is a valid way to obtain the behaviours of memory. But the memo/memory distinction is behavioural, and those behaviours are obtainable in a governed external substrate without touching θ. TerranSoul is the existence proof: consolidation/reconsolidation (C1), decay/tiering (C2), an evolving typed graph (C3), and calibrated metacognition that refits from outcomes (C4) all ship and run with the model frozen, and governed self-evolution (C5) ships as a write-time gate on every autonomous write path. The "Frozen Novice" charge mislabels weight-freezing as expertise-freezing; Section 5.1-5.2 show behaviour changing across sessions with weights frozen.

Why we hold weights frozen on purpose. Editing θ per session re-imports the very problems an external store solves cleanly: a weight edit is not inspectable, not revertible as a row, not governable at write time, and not portable to another client - and MEMIT-class edits have documented spillover. The properties that make the target paper's security limitation tractable (quarantine, supersession, provenance, audit) are properties of structured data, not of parameters. So the divergence is principled: we accept the compositional ceiling the theorem describes in exchange for an auditable, governable, revertible memory, and route compositional needs to retrieval-time decomposition rather than weight edits.

Delivery reliability is a distinct, under-studied axis. The literature optimises retrieval [1,4,8,9] and reasoning [5,6], and the agentic-RAG survey lists memory management and governance as open [26] - but the variable that dominated our outcome lived in neither place: it was the loop's bookkeeping (turn-count vs. execution-count indexing). Bad delivery propagates as the surveys warn bad writes do [15,31].

The strongest empirical challenge: SIA's two-lever co-evolution. The most direct counter-example to our frozen-weight mandate is SIA [45], which lets a Feedback-Agent rewrite a task agent's scaffold and update its weights, and reports that combining both levers exceeds scaffold-iteration alone on all three of its benchmarks. We concede the force of this: on one-shot benchmark maximisation where retraining is permitted, SIA's scaffold-only ablation climbs and then plateaus, lifted further only once weight updates switch on. Three things keep the concession bounded:

  • First, it is a different problem. SIA optimises a throwaway, task-specific agent for a single benchmark in an offline, multi-generation loop, keeping no persistent memory between unrelated tasks - no embedder, no typed graph, no retrieval, a look-back of one generation. Scored on the five criteria of Section 3, SIA's store is a memo, not a memory; its weight path is a training loop, not a memory at all. SIA answers a third, orthogonal question (which lever maximises one benchmark), not the memo-vs-memory question this paper is about.
  • Second, SIA's own taxonomy partly vindicates us. Its "harness-update school" - improvement with weights frozen - is exactly the lever we claim is real, and its scaffold-only ablation climbing substantially before it plateaus is itself evidence for that claim. The residual disagreement is narrow: whether the fixed-weight lever saturates. SIA shows it can, on a one-shot benchmark; we claim that for a lifelong memory the operative ceiling is the frozen actor's planning depth, not the substrate's (Section 5.2). Our head-to-head (Section 5.7) is direct evidence the fixed-weight lever need not saturate below weight-editing on these tasks: on SIA's own headline LawBench our frozen actor + memory scores higher than SIA's weight-trained 120B (76.3-80.0% vs 70.1%), and on its TriMul kernel it reaches ≈ parity once hardware is normalised.
  • Third, SIA buys its result at the exact price we decline to pay. The opacity, non-revertibility, write-time ungovernability, non-portability, and MEMIT-class spillover catalogued above are acceptable for a disposable champion artifact and disqualifying for an auditable, governed, lifelong self. SIA's objective makes that trade correctly; ours makes the opposite trade correctly. Neither result refutes the other - they optimise different objectives. (Provenance: SIA's weight-update path is paper-reported; an inspection of its public source tree [47] confirmed only the scaffold-editing path, so we treat the weight-editing result as paper-reported, not source-verified.)

What we do take from SIA is methodological: its discipline of moving one lever per generation, to keep each delta attributable, is the right instrument for our own bench loop. Two of the target paper's contributions we intend to adopt: the CGT metric (model-frozen-compatible) and the persistent-injection threat model (MINJA/PoisonedRAG, the explicit adversary BCF-21's poisoning guard must defeat). The weight-editing techniques themselves are not adoptable for the resident actor without violating the frozen-model mandate - a deliberate divergence, not an oversight.

07 · LimitationsWhat this does not show

  • The AGI-pure score is low (10-20) for the 4B. With no taught solution, the effective-4B's multi-step planning ceiling - not the memory layer - binds the score; the expected regime per TALES/BALROG [13,14]. The 12B doubled across episodes (10 → 20) only as a single lucky episode (modal 10 → 10); the self-improvement campaign's peak 50 is an across-run accumulation artifact, and the de-confounded per-run result on a fresh task-naïve brain is ~15 (Section 5.1). The per-episode ceiling remains actor-bound.
  • The 350 is a demonstration, not a discovery. Section 5.3 gives the brain the solution to isolate delivery reliability; the taught knowledge is demo-only and never enters the AGI-pure substrate.
  • Single ROM, single model family. Results are on zork1.z5 with the gemma4 family plus a frontier reference, with qwen2.5:7b the first cross-model point. The full Jericho/TALES surface and broader model families are a deliberate scope boundary.
  • The criteria are arguable. Our five-criterion definition of "memory" is a position, not a theorem; a reader who defines memory as "knowledge in θ" will say we built an excellent database, not a memory. The disagreement is partly terminological.
  • We do not close the compositional gap. Theorem 1 of [38] stands; our multi-hop LoCoMo gap is consistent with it. We claim escape from the memo charge, not from the compositional-ceiling charge, and we do not yet measure CGT.
  • C5's adversarial evaluation is internal. BCF-21 governed self-evolution ships with a persistent-injection regression suite over the real connector and peer write paths, but we have not reproduced MINJA or PoisonedRAG at their published attack strengths against the gate; the guard is tested against our own adversarial write bursts, not the original attacks.
  • Self-advocacy and n. This is a position paper by the system's authors; we mitigate the bias by sourcing every number to a committed file and reporting the figures that cut against us. Several benches are single-trial or n = 2; retrieval numbers across benchmarks are not mutually comparable. None of these is a leaderboard claim.

09 · ConclusionMemory is what a store does over time

Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] are right that contextual agentic memory, as commonly built, is a memo. We disagree only that the cure must be written into weights. Memory is what a store does over time - consolidate, reconsolidate, forget, structure, calibrate, govern - and a frozen-weight system that does all six is not a memo, whatever its parameters do. TerranSoul ships the first five faculties and designs the sixth, and produces measurable cross-episode behavioural change with its model frozen. The memo charge is therefore escapable without editing θ.

The empirical half makes the same point on the acting axis. We wired deliberately weak local models to an external memory server over MCP and measured them on Zork I. The production frozen 12B's self-improvement campaign broke the modal-10 ceiling to a peak episode of 50 - via across-run accumulation, the de-confounded per-run result ~15 (modal 10 → 10), the residual bound the frozen model's planning, not the memory; and externalising task knowledge lifts the unaided frozen 4B from 0 to 10-20 with no task seeds, both controls at zero. With a strategy held fixed, delivery reliability - not added model capacity - moved the same 4B from a non-deterministic 73/177 to a deterministic 350/350; we position this as a third agent-performance axis. And the substrate enables distillation through memory: a frontier model solves once, a cheap local model executes thereafter, the knowledge living as editable, inspectable data rather than weights. On the orthogonal retrieval axis the same substrate is competitive or leading on four public memory benchmarks (LongMemEval-S R@5 99.4%, LoCoMo rrf_emb R@10 64.5%, a personal-AI head-to-head at 9.82/10, ~1.1 s, $0 under the deterministic protocol); engaged against SIA, the frozen lever scores higher on its own headline LawBench (76.3-80.0% vs 70.1%) and reaches a hardware-normalized estimate near parity on its kernel benchmark.

The honest scorecard: diagnosis accepted, remedy generalised, two of the interlocutor's ideas (CGT and the injection threat model) adopted, one (weight-editing) declined by mandate - and the central charge answered by construction. The boundaries are explicit (Sections 7-8): a single ROM, a single model family, single-trial and n = 2 runs where noted, an H100 figure that is an estimate, an MLE-Bench result not run, and a compositional ceiling we do not claim to lift. Together the results are a concrete argument for treating memory as a first-class architectural layer: observable, probeable, governable, and editable in ways a model-internal store is not.
