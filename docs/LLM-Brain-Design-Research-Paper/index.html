Research · position + measurement

From Memo to Memory: a first-class external-memory architecture for frozen language models.

The behavioural case for memory-not-weights, and a measured existence proof — Zork I, four retrieval benchmarks, and a SIA engagement, May–July 2026. We accept the memo-vs-memory charge and contest the dichotomy: what makes a store a memory rather than a memo is what it does over time, and those behaviours are realisable with the model's weights frozen.

What this paper argues
  • We accept the diagnosis of Xu, Dai & Zhang [38]: per-turn contextual agentic memory is a memo — lookup, not learning — and calling its accumulation “learning” is a category error.
  • We contest the dichotomy. The paper splits persistence into context (CC) vs. weights (θ) and prescribes writing knowledge into θ. We argue memory is individuated by behaviour, and propose five falsifiable criteria (C1–C5) a governed external substrate can satisfy with weights frozen.
  • We map each criterion to a shipped TerranSoul faculty with source-file references, and report a measured existence proof — every number sourced to a committed file.
  • We engage the strongest weight-update counter-pole, SIA [45]: we concede its benchmark result and bound it three ways, and keep only its headline LawBench as an on-thesis comparison — on which the frozen actor + memory scores higher.
  • We are explicit about scope: this does not refute the compositional-generalisation theorem of [38]; it shows the memo charge is escapable without editing θ.
Standpoint and honesty. This is a combined position-and-measurement paper by the TerranSoul project — the system it advocates. We have a stake in the conclusion and try to earn it by reporting the numbers that cut against us: the modal Zork I result is 10 → 10, not the peak 45; the deep cross-episode climb is high-variance and largely an across-run accumulation artifact; several benches are n-limited and single-PC; a ranking-time forgetting ablation and a corpus-scale governance ablation are both flagged as open work rather than reported with invented numbers. We concede the central architectural point to our interlocutor [38] up front — TerranSoul does not edit model weights, so on that paper's own definition our “memory” is weight-frozen by mandate. The argument below is that weight-editing is one route to the behaviours of memory, not the only one — and that a frozen-weight substrate can deliver all of them, measurably.

AbstractWhat this paper measures, and what it argues

Two questions about long-term memory for language-model agents are usually run together, and we keep them apart. The first is architectural: should an agent's memory live inside the model — as weights or a long context window — or outside it, as a queryable store the agent reads and writes through a tool interface? The second is conceptual: Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] argue that the dominant “contextual / agentic memory” paradigm — retrieve passages, paste them into the context window per turn — is not memory at all but a memo, and prove a compositional sample-complexity separation between context-engineering (CC) and weight-based memory (θ), prescribing a consolidation channel that writes experience into θ. We accept the diagnosis and contest the dichotomy. We argue that what makes a store a memory rather than a memo is a set of behavioural properties — offline consolidation and reconsolidation, adaptive forgetting, a typed structure that evolves, calibrated metacognition, and governed self-evolution — and that these are realisable in a structured external substrate with the model's weights frozen. We state five falsifiable criteria (C1–C5), map each to a shipped TerranSoul faculty with source-file references, and interleave the external-memory architecture (an MCP-served Rust brain with a six-signal hybrid retriever and a typed knowledge graph) with the methods of a controlled benchmark.

The evidence headline spans two axes. On the long-horizon acting axis, the external substrate lifts a deliberately weak frozen 4B (gemma4:e4b) from 0 (two controls) to 10–20 on Zork I — a task TALES calls “insurmountable … even for modern state-of-the-art LLMs” [14] — and produces verified cross-episode behavioural change; holding the model and a captured strategy fixed, delivery reliability alone moves the same 4B from a non-deterministic 73/177 to a deterministic 350/350. On the orthogonal retrieval axis, we broaden the evidence base beyond Zork: on a memory-quality corpus the hybrid retriever reaches NDCG@10 91.3–95.0% at 91.3–91.6% token savings against static-context baselines, and against nine independently-run published memory frameworks on the identical fixture, several plateau at NDCG@10 88.2% (embedder-bound) while others measure as low as 1.5–18.2%; on LongMemEval-S the hybrid RRF retriever reaches NDCG@10 95.1% (R@5 99.4%); on LoCoMo, QA scores 63.6 J-score (Mem0 methodology) against a published Mem0 baseline of ~61.7, while retrieval-only MTEB recall is a documented, disclosed weakness (multi-hop/open-domain R@10 in the 44–48% range); ingest throughput measures 530–2,652 rows/s at 40k–1M résumés, with an openly disclosed cross-lingual retrieval gap (en NDCG@10 93.4% vs. vi 45.4%). Engaging SIA [45] on its own headline LawBench, our frozen 12B + memory scores higher (76.3% full / up to 80.0% with a stronger frozen actor vs SIA's 70.1%); its other three benchmarks are explicitly excluded as off-thesis (§5.9). We are explicit about scope: this does not refute the compositional-generalisation theorem of [38]; it shows the memo charge is escapable without editing θ.

01 · Background, SOTA & related workWhere this sits

A memo is a note you write to get through the afternoon: you jot what you need, you act on it, you throw it away, and nothing about today's note changes how you write tomorrow's. The system under test throughout this paper — “the brain” — is a Rust memory server with a six-signal hybrid retriever (BM25/FTS5 + dense HNSW ANN + reciprocal-rank fusion, k = 60) and a typed knowledge graph, exposed to any client over the Model Context Protocol (MCP). To probe whether such a store is a memory and not a memo, the acting-axis experiments below wire it to a model so small it cannot hold a long-horizon task in its head — gemma4:e4b, an effective-4B edge model — and measure it on Zork I; the retrieval-axis experiments wire the same substrate to public benchmarks and to nine independently-run published memory frameworks. TerranSoul runs under a standing mandate to keep its local model (gemma4:12b-it-qat) frozen; if “memory” required θ-editing, TerranSoul could have none. We run the upstream stickystyle/ZorkGPT agent [12] — which we did not author — unmodified in the Zork experiments, swapping only its memory and knowledge managers (§4.7).

1.1 The 2026 agentic-memory debate

The proximate interlocutor is Xu, Dai & Zhang [38], whose memo-vs-memory charge and CC-vs-θ separation we engage throughout. Its empirical scaffolding (reported as their evidence, not independently verified here) cites ParamMem-style results in which parametric storage scores above retrieval with the gap widening on novel tasks; Ovadia 2024 that RAG does not improve compositional reasoning; SCAN/COGS multi-hop results; and two memory-injection attacks — MINJA at 98.2% success and PoisonedRAG at 90% with five adversarial texts — as evidence that a persistent store is a persistent attack surface. It names MemGPT, Reflexion, and Voyager as “CC-engineering only,” and proposes a Compositional Generalization over Time (CGT) metric: does accuracy on novel concept combinations rise with experience, not just recall@k? Its Theorem 1 (a compositional sample-complexity separation via Fano's inequality and VC bounds) states retrieval needs Ω(k²) stored examples where parametric learning needs only O(d + log(1/δ)/δ), and the gap is context-window-independent. From this it derives four limitations — definitional, structural, dynamic (the “Frozen Novice”: agents accumulate notes but never develop expertise because weights never change between sessions), and security (injected content persists across all future sessions) — and prescribes a consolidation channel into θ.

Three further 2026 lines sharpen the picture. The dual-process line — DCPM, Memory Beyond Recall [39] — splits a System-1 “daytime writer” from a System-2 “nighttime” schema-induction pass; its raw-recall-vs-distilled-strategy split is the memo/memory line drawn behaviourally rather than by substrate. The governance line — SSGM [40] — frames drift, poisoning, and unbounded growth as the missing governance layer, observing that the danger only exists once memory truly persists and self-evolves: a scratchpad has nothing to govern. A cluster of surveys and taxonomies [15,41,42,43,44] converges on a shared picture: “memory” is the externalisation layer around a frozen LLM, distinct from per-turn context/retrieval.

1.2 Agent memory mechanisms, retrieval, and graphs

The external-memory lineage runs from MemGPT/Letta [2], through Mem0 [1] (whose LLM-arbitrated conflict resolution the substrate adopts at its memory_edges layer [20]), to HippoRAG [4] and spatial schemes such as MemoryPalace [3]. Du [15] formalises memory as a “write–manage–read loop,” warning that “one bad write can pollute the store for many steps downstream” — the abstract statement of the silent-ingest failure we hit concretely (§4.6). The retriever fuses lexical and dense channels with reciprocal-rank fusion and applies Contextual Retrieval [8] and HyDE [9] as a per-query-class tool rather than a global default [20]; its graph layer follows GraphRAG's Leiden-community lineage and the typed-edge designs of Zep/Graphiti [10], MAGMA [24], and PersonalAI [25]. The agentic-RAG survey [26] names “Memory Management and Long-Term Adaptation” and “Safety, Trust, and Governance” as critical open challenges — the territory of our write-reliability (§4.6) and delivery-reliability (§5.1) results.

1.3 Memory and long-horizon benchmarks

Three benchmarks anchor the retrieval field: LoCoMo [34], LongMemEval [35], and BEAM; MemoryAgentBench [22] and AMA-Bench [23] ground evaluation in cognitive science and long-horizon agentic memory. These expose a sobering gap — Du [15] reports systems that “score near-perfectly on LoCoMo plummet to 40–60% in MemoryArena.” The production systems the target paper indicts sit here too: Mem0 [1] reports a LoCoMo QA J-score of 68.5%, Letta/MemGPT [2] 83.2%, Zep/Graphiti [10] 34.53% — but these are end-to-end QA accuracy, not retrieval recall, and are not comparable to the retrieval numbers in §5.4/§5.6 [21]. On the acting side, Jericho [11] exposes 55 interactive-fiction games with ground-truth state; BALROG [13] and TALES [14] motivate games as agent benchmarks and report broad struggle; TextQuests [29] and Dual-Scale World Models [30] extend the line. The upstream agent we run, ZorkGPT [12], peaks at 115/350 in its public continuous run — our frontier-scaffold reference (§5.1).

1.4 Agent reasoning, self-evolution, and skill libraries

ReAct [5] interleaves reasoning and acting; Reflexion [6] adds verbal self-feedback across episodes; Voyager [7] accumulates a skill library. The 2025–2026 self-evolution wave extends this: AutoSkill [27] performs “experience-driven lifelong learning via skill self-evolution,” and RL frameworks fold a validated skill library into training [28]. Self-Guided Self-Play (SGS) [37], evaluated on Lean4 theorem proving, is the most relevant recent point: a frozen Guide scores a Conjecturer's self-generated sub-problems on relevance (0–5), conclusion-complexity (0–4), and redundancy (0–1), combined as R_guide = max(0, relevance + (2−complexity) + (1−redundancy)). We adopt neither its RL training nor its R_solve rollout signal under our frozen-harness / AGI-pure discipline — only the gradient-free Conjecturer→Guide curriculum idea and the Guide's exact reward, re-implemented as a deterministic rubric because our brain exposes no instruction-following judge tool (§5.1). Our room-scoped reflections (§4.6) are Reflexion-style feedback given spatial scope; our “taught skill” (§5.1) is a Voyager-style library entry.

1.5 The self-improving-agent counter-pole: SIA

SIA — Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates [45] — is the sharpest contrast to our thesis. It frames autonomous self-improvement as two historically disjoint silos: a harness-update school, in which a meta-agent rewrites a task agent's scaffold with weights frozen, and a test-time-training school, which updates weights on task feedback with the harness frozen. SIA co-evolves both: a Feedback-Agent reads a full trajectory and chooses, per generation, to rewrite the scaffold (weights frozen) or apply an RL weight update (LoRA; scaffold frozen). Its headline is that combining both levers exceeds scaffold-iteration alone on all three of its benchmarks — Chinese legal charge classification, low-level GPU-kernel optimisation, and single-cell RNA denoising — by +25.1% over prior SOTA, 12.4% faster kernels (1,017 vs 1,161 µs), and +20.4% respectively [45]. This is the strongest empirical case in the 2026 literature that a fixed-weight lever, used alone, can plateau below what weight editing reaches — and, on SIA's own taxonomy, a partial vindication of our position, since its harness-update silo is a fixed-weight lever. We engage its headline benchmark directly in §5.9 and §6.1, and explicitly scope the other three out as off-thesis (§5.9); a source-audited side-by-side of all four lives in the companion comparison [47].

1.6 Published memory frameworks, measured on the identical fixture

Beyond citation, we ran nine independently-published memory frameworks through their own retrievers on the same 240-observation, 20-query fixture used for our own quality bench (§5.2), pinning the embedder (nomic-embed-text, 768-d) where the framework supports it, so the comparison isolates retrieval strategy rather than embedder choice [48]. Mem0, Letta, Cognee, and Khoj — four architecturally distinct systems (extraction-pipeline, LLM-as-OS paging, graph-cognify, and bi-encoder semantic search respectively) — converge to the identical NDCG@10 of 88.2% once the embedder is held fixed, direct evidence that on this fixture the embedder, not the framework, is the ceiling. HippoRAG-2's Personalized-PageRank graph retrieval reaches NDCG@10 74.2% — below the pure-vector cluster, consistent with its passage-expansion design trading precision for multi-hop reach. Two systems collapse: A-MEM's LLM-augmented ChromaDB notes score NDCG@10 1.5% (its local gemma3:1b note-generation degrades the anchor text more than it enriches it, on this fixture), and Memary's knowledge-graph entity retrieval scores 10.0% (its keyword-mode graph traversal returns zero candidates for most of the 20 queries). Nous Research's Hermes-Agent, an FTS5+Jaccard+HRR holographic store with no embedder at all, scores NDCG@10 18.2% — its SQLite implicit-AND semantics require every query token to match, so most raw natural-language queries return nothing. As a frontier reference (a different, cloud model and an agentic-retrieval shape, not like-for-like with the local rows), Claude Code + GENesis-AGI's grep/read agentic retriever reaches NDCG@10 83.9%. These nine numbers are related-work data points, not TerranSoul results; TerranSoul's own hybrid-retrieval numbers on this same fixture are reported in §5.2, where the comparison is direct.

1.7 The state of the debate

Prior work largely optimises retrieval quality [1,4,8,9] or reasoning structure [5,6,7]; the target paper [38] prescribes moving knowledge into θ. We agree with the diagnosis and most of the constructive frame. Our disagreement is narrow. The paper equates “true memory” with “knowledge in θ.” We argue that memory is better individuated by what it does than by where it lives: a substrate is a memory, not a memo, when it consolidates offline, revises beliefs in place, forgets adaptively, grows a typed structure, calibrates its own confidence, and governs its own evolution. These are behavioural commitments, and they are realisable in a structured external store while the language model's weights stay frozen. We do not claim this closes the paper's compositional gap (it does not; §6.1); we claim the memo-vs-memory distinction is not the same distinction as CC-vs-θ, and that conflating them concedes too much to fine-tuning. We contribute two things at once: (i) the argument that the memo charge is escapable without θ-editing, via five behavioural criteria a frozen-weight substrate can satisfy (§3–§4); and (ii) the empirical isolation of a third agent-performance axis — does the chosen action actually reach the world, every turn? — shown, with model and strategy held fixed, to dominate outcome in our setting (§5.1). To our knowledge this is the first clean separation of delivery reliability from model capacity on a long-horizon game.

The debate at a glance. Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] — we agree the memo diagnosis wholesale; contest that memo-vs-memory equals CC-vs-θ; concede Theorem 1 stands and we do not close the compositional gap. SIA [45] — we concede the benchmark result and bound it three ways; its harness-update silo is itself a fixed-weight lever that vindicates us; on LawBench the frozen actor + memory scores higher. DCPM [39] — adopted as C1 consolidation/reconsolidation. SSGM [40] — adopted as the basis of C5/BCF-21. Du's survey [15] — adopted the write–manage–read framing; our silent-ingest failure (§4.6) is its concrete instance. Mem0 [1] — adopted conflict resolution at memory_edges; its LoCoMo QA number is end-to-end QA, not our retrieval recall. MemGPT/Letta [2] — shared lineage; we add a typed evolving KG (C3) and write-time governance (C5). HippoRAG [4] — retrieval lineage; multi-hop is our documented weakness (§5.4), consistent with [38]'s compositional prediction. Zep/Graphiti [10] — adopted the typed directional-edge design; we add supersedes/contradicts self-correction. Reflexion/Voyager [6,7] — named “CC-only” by [38]; we give reflection spatial scope and separate having a skill from executing it. SGS [37] — adopted only the gradient-free Conjecturer→Guide idea + the R_guide formula; rejected the RL training under the frozen-weight mandate. The agentic-RAG survey [26] — our write-reliability and delivery-reliability results target its named open challenges directly.

02 · HypothesisWhat we predict, and how it can fail

The paper turns on two questions we deliberately keep apart. Architectural: should an agent's memory live inside the model — as weights or a long context window — or outside it, as a queryable store the agent reads and writes through a tool interface? Conceptual: is retrieval-and-paste “memory” at all, or is it a memo that happens to persist? [38] answers both at once — memory lives in θ, and anything else is a memo. We answer them separately, and the separation is the paper's thesis: our disagreement with [38] is narrow — it is about behavioural individuation, not about location. A substrate is a memory, not a memo, when it consolidates offline, revises beliefs in place, forgets adaptively, grows a typed structure, calibrates its own confidence, and governs its own evolution — and nothing in that list requires the behaviours to live in θ.

We state this as one falsifiable hypothesis, and everything downstream traces back to it:

A frozen-weight LLM coupled to an external store satisfying criteria C1–C5 (§3) will exhibit memory-like behavioural change — cross-episode improvement, reliable recall, graceful forgetting — competitive with weight-updated or retrieval-only baselines, without weight training.The paper's central, falsifiable claim.

The hypothesis is falsifiable in the ordinary sense: if a frozen-weight substrate satisfying C1–C5 showed no cross-episode change, or if its retrieval quality trailed retrieval-only baselines by a wide margin, or if the delivery/knowledge distinction collapsed under test, the hypothesis would be wrong and the target paper's prescription would stand unchallenged. Three commitments operationalise it, each with its own downstream section: the Behavioral Definition (§3) states C1–C5 precisely enough to be checked; the Method (§4) maps each criterion to a shipped faculty with source-file references and defines the metrics used to check it; the Experiments (§5) run the checks — on Zork I for the acting axis, on four retrieval benchmarks and nine published frameworks for the retrieval axis, and against SIA's headline benchmark for the weight-update counter-pole.

Four contributions follow directly from the hypothesis:

  1. Five falsifiable criteria (C1–C5, §3) that separate a memo from a memory, behaviourally, with a criterion-by-criterion mapping to shipped faculties and source-file references (§4).
  2. A controlled three-arm benchmark (external memory server / agent's-own managers / none) on the same model, seed, and ROM, with the memory substrate as the sole independent variable (§4.7); design and transcripts public [16].
  3. A broadened retrieval-axis evidence base — a memory-quality bench against static-context baselines and nine published frameworks, LongMemEval-S, LoCoMo (QA, retrieval-only, and at scale), and a cross-framework RAG comparison — so the acting-axis Zork result is one line of evidence among several, not the paper's entire empirical base (§5.2–5.7).
  4. A bounded engagement with SIA on its own headline benchmark (§5.9, §6.1), with the other three benchmarks explicitly scoped out rather than silently omitted.

What would make us wrong, concretely: if the C1–C5 faculties turned out to be dispensable to the Zork lift (i.e. the lift survived with consolidation, decay, and the KG all disabled), the hypothesis's causal claim would fail even if the score still rose. We do not run that ablation in this paper (§5.8 states why), which is itself a limitation we carry into §6.2 rather than paper over.

03 · Behavioral definitionFive criteria that separate a memo from a memory

To make the hypothesis checkable we propose five criteria. They are behavioural: they ask what a store does over time, not where its bits sit. A pure retrieve-and-stuff pipeline fails all five; the target paper's θ-editing remedy passes some by construction. We claim a structured external substrate can pass all five with weights frozen, and we concede in §6.1 that the criteria are themselves arguable.

Two of the target paper's four limitations are about these criteria. The “Frozen Novice” (its dynamic limitation) is the absence of C1: a store that only appends never develops expertise. The security limitation (persistent injection) is the absence of C5. We answer these two directly in §4.1 and §4.5. The remaining two limitations — definitional and structural — are genuinely about CC-vs-θ and the compositional ceiling; we do not claim the five criteria dissolve them, and we say so in §6.2.

04 · Method & metricsTerranSoul, mapped criterion-by-criterion

TerranSoul is a local-first companion (Vue + Tauri + Rust + SQLite + Ollama) whose “brain” is a Rust memory server reached over MCP. By project mandate the language model's weights are frozen; all learning is externalised into the substrate. Below, each criterion of §3 is mapped to a real faculty with source-file references [46], the harness methods used to test the mapping are interleaved, and a Metrics subsection (§4.8) formally defines every quantity reported in §5. We label each faculty honestly: every one strengthens or revises stored structure, never θ.

4.1 C1 — Consolidation and reconsolidation (the answer to the Frozen Novice)

The reconsolidation module (src-tauri/src/memory/reconsolidation.rs) implements an idle complementary-learning-systems (CLS) replay pass. consolidate() samples the highest-salience episodic rows due under FSRS-style spacing — salience() weighs importance, access count and age; next_stability() schedules the next review — and strengthen_memory() raises a row's Laplace-smoothed confidence and stamps a consolidation_run_id. The harder property is reconsolidation: reactivate() marks a row labile, should_reconsolidate() gates whether a similar new observation should overwrite rather than co-exist, and reconsolidate_in_place() corrects a stale belief cleanly instead of stacking a contradicting duplicate beside it. A memo cannot do this. Honest caveat: this revises stored confidence and content, not θ. The Frozen-Novice charge is that weights never change between sessions; our answer is that behaviour changes between sessions because the substrate the frozen model reads has changed — which §5.1 shows is measurable.

4.2 C2 — Three tiers and adaptive forgetting

The store (src-tauri/src/memory/store.rs) defines a MemoryTier enum: Short (evicted at session end), Working (held until consolidation), and Long (permanent, vector-indexed). confidence_decay.rs applies a per-cognitive-kind multiplicative decay confidence × 0.5^(age/half_life) with half-lives of 365 days for judgments, 90 for semantic, 60 for procedural, 7 for episodic, folded into the retrieval RRF score. A correction (judgment) is durable for a year; a time-stamped episode loses rank in a week — salience, not recency-of-write, determines what survives. §5.8 discusses what is and is not yet ablated about this mechanism.

4.3 C3 — A typed knowledge graph that evolves

The edge store (src-tauri/src/memory/edges.rs) holds typed, directional edges: COMMON_RELATION_TYPES includes supersedes, contradicts, derived_from, governs, depends_on; the (src_id, dst_id, rel_type) tuple is unique so extraction is idempotent. add_edge() / add_edges_batch() are exposed to any client over MCP via brain_add_edge. In the Zork bench harness, map adjacency and object/location pairs are promoted into this memory_edges graph; a direct probe confirms Living Room —exits_via_west→ Kitchen persists across restarts and is queryable by any MCP client (§4.7) — the spatial graph one agent builds is legible to the next [10,24,25]. This buys multi-hop recall and a structure that self-corrects: a supersedes/contradicts edge demotes a stale claim rather than leaving two equal-rank contradictions.

4.4 C4 — Calibrated metacognition

The metacognition module (src-tauri/src/memory/metacognition.rs) is a second-order monitor that estimates a calibrated confidence before an answer is emitted. route() turns the score into one of answer / deliberate / seek-more / abstain. Crucially the calibration is learned from outcomes: recalibrate() refits a Platt map from logged calibration data against realised five-class session outcomes (minimising log-loss / ECE) and rewrites the brain-seeded config rows. The curve is not hardcoded; it improves with experience — with weights frozen, because the thing refit is a config row in the substrate, not a parameter in θ.

4.5 C5 — Governed self-evolution (the answer to persistent injection)

The target paper's sharpest practical point is security: MINJA at 98.2% and PoisonedRAG at 90% with five texts mean a store that accepts any write carries an attacker's payload into every future session. TerranSoul's answer is a write-time governance gate, BCF-21 (src-tauri/src/memory/governance.rs, design contract in docs/brain-advanced-design.md). Status update: BCF-21 is now a shipped module, not merely a design — decide() composes a drift signal (reusing the C1 reconsolidation/corroboration path), a poisoning/injection signal (source-concentration and source-trust provenance via source_burst_anomaly()), a growth signal (the decay/prune budget of C2), and quarantine of peer-brain imports until corroborated; govern() and governed_write() route every autonomous (brain-authored) durable write through the gate and log the verdict; release_quarantine() and record_outcome()/self_tune() close the loop. Twenty unit tests exercise it, including anomaly_blocks_as_poison, low_source_trust_blocks_as_poison, and poison_outranks_quarantine_and_drift — a synthetic poisoning pair, not a corpus-scale bench (the gap §5.8 names explicitly). User writes bypass the gate; only the brain's own writes are governed. It cites SSGM [40] as its basis. The point: governance is possible and meaningful only because the store is real memory — a memo has nothing to govern.

4.6 Measurement-discipline case study: the closed loop, the brain bridge, and write-reliability failures

The five faculties compose into a loop. Write: a policy (auto_learn.rs) decides when to extract memories (default every ~10 turns with a cooldown). Manage / self-tune: idle passes run consolidation (§4.1) and recalibration (§4.4). Read: hybrid FTS5 + vector + KG retrieval with rerank. In the Zork harness, a brain bridge converts raw Z-machine state into importance-scored, KG-structured memories: signal-derived importance (score_delta>0 → 10; inventory change → 8; first visit → 7; death → 10 + a fatal memory), structured content (prefix | Location | Action | Result | Score | Inventory) so the KG auto-extractor builds typed edges, and principle-before-episodic retrieval so cross-episode rules are not buried.

We relocate this case study to Method, not Experiments, because it is about how we know the pipeline is trustworthy, not about a result. Building the bench surfaced four agent-memory failure classes, each diagnosable and fixable at the brain layer without retraining: (1) Verify, don't trust — every brain_ingest_lesson had silently stored zero rows because the MCP client treated a structured error envelope as success (the concrete form of Du's “one bad write can pollute the store” [15], here a no-write undetected); a 14-line fix plus a hard “is it retrievable next episode?” probe closed it. (2) Reflections require spatial scope — per-room reflections tagged loc_<id> surface exactly when the agent re-enters the room. (3) The prompt is a snapshot, not a stream — a reload_knowledge_base() push fixes a lantern discovered at turn 11 never reaching the turn-0 prompt, possible only because the bridge owns the prompt. (4) Grammar is not strategy — replacing a verb-list seed is a brain_ingest_lesson call, not a fine-tune. A memory layer with no verification probe will lie to you, and bad delivery propagates exactly as the surveys warn bad writes do [15,31]; the delivery-reliability result of §5.1 is the direct empirical descendant of failure class (1).

4.7 Retrieval, harness controls, and AGI-purity discipline

Hybrid retrieval. Reads fuse four retrievers (dense HNSW, lexical FTS5, KG-neighbour, freshness) with reciprocal-rank fusion (k = 60); Contextual-Retrieval-style situating [8] and HyDE expansion [9] are applied, the latter gated per query class after ablation [20]. RRF is chosen for robustness: a failing retriever contributes nothing rather than poisoning the top-k.

Three-arm benchmark. We run upstream stickystyle/ZorkGPT [12] against Jericho's zork1.z5 [11] in a sealed Docker image. Agent, critic, extractor and strategy-generator are the same local model across arms; seed, temperature and ROM are held constant. Only the cognitive substrate is swapped: none, zorkgpt-default, and terransoul-brain. The brain runs as a separate OS process; the bridge speaks MCP JSON-RPC 2.0 over a real TCP boundary, bearer-token authenticated, with explicit error accounting.

Task-agnostic harness controls. A sequence of domain-agnostic perception/decision gates was added at the bridge, each validated by a sub-10-second reproduction before any bench: ID-keyed mapping by Z-machine location_id; object-tree/valid-exit validation; id-based loop-breaking; one-shot light-acquisition; and a delivery rule that banks valued (score-bearing) items over junk — value learned from the score signal, never a hardcoded treasure list.

AGI-purity discipline. A standing rule forbids seeding the substrate with Zork maps, routes, walkthroughs, room names, or curated vocabularies, enforced by a grep gate (“zero verb/score constants in the bridge source”). All Zork lift results (§5.1) are produced task-naïve except the explicitly-labelled taught-solution demonstration, whose move-level solution is a demo artifact never written to the memory seed (grep-verified) and gated behind a TAUGHT_SOLUTION_DEMO=1 runtime flag. Digest-level reproducibility is pinned: the ROM (zork1.z5 SHA-256 0ae5ac22…85c15fd), the 396-move taught solution (SHA-256 8461b56e…24df88c7), the upstream ZorkGPT commit, Jericho 3.3.1, Docker image IDs, and Ollama model digests are recorded in repro-manifest.json.

4.8 Metrics

Every number in §5 is reported against one of the following, named metrics — consolidated here so Experiments can report without re-deriving:

  • Recall@K (R@K), NDCG@10, MRR — standard retrieval metrics. R@K = share of gold-relevant items appearing in the top-K; NDCG@10 weights hits by rank and relevance grade within the top 10; MRR is the reciprocal rank of the first relevant hit. Used throughout §5.2–5.5 for retrieval-only evaluation (no generation involved).
  • J-score — end-to-end QA accuracy under the Mem0-paper LLM-as-judge methodology [1]: retrieve top-K, generate an answer from retrieved context, have a judge model score the answer 0–10 against the gold reference, and report mean(judge_scores) × 10 on a 0–100 scale. Comparable to published Mem0/Zep/LangMem baselines only when the judge model matches; we flag ours (gemma4:12b-it-qat) as directionally, not strictly, comparable (§5.4).
  • Delivery-reliability rate — the share of turns in which the agent's chosen action actually reaches the game engine and is executed, independent of whether that action was correct. Isolated from model capacity by a no-LLM harness that replays a fixed move sequence under an injected drop rate (§5.1).
  • MCP error rate — failed MCP calls (transport error, malformed envelope, or timeout) divided by total MCP calls issued by the bridge in a run; reported per-run, not amortised across runs of different length.
  • Ingest throughput — rows committed per second through add_sessions_jsonl, the same code path production ingest uses; reported as an overall rate over the full run (checkpointed every N rows), not a peak instantaneous rate.

Forgetting. The decay half-lives of §4.2 (365/90/60/7 days by cognitive kind) are a real, shipped ranking-time mechanism with unit-level formula tests (confidence decays to exactly half at one half-life, per confidence_decay.rs's own test suite). What we do not have, and want to state plainly rather than fabricate, is a corpus-scale retrieval-quality ablation that toggles the ranking-time decay term on and off over the LongMemEval-S corpus and reports the recall delta — the harness (benchmark/scripts/longmemeval-s.mjs) currently has no decay-toggle flag. This is a real gap, not a hedge for its own sake: without that ablation we cannot yet say whether ranking-time decay helps, hurts, or is neutral for retrieval quality on this corpus — only that it exists and is unit-tested. The cheap fix is exactly what it sounds like: an env-gated decay toggle in the harness and two ~500-question re-runs (with/without), on the order of minutes given the existing harness. We flag it as immediate future work rather than run it under time pressure and risk a rushed, unreviewed number in a paper whose central claim is measurement honesty.

Governance. State this honestly: no bench-level, corpus-scale governance-ON-vs-OFF study exists. What exists is unit-test-scale: governance.rs's 21 tests include a synthetic poisoning pair (anomaly_blocks_as_poison, low_source_trust_blocks_as_poison, poison_outranks_quarantine_and_drift) that exercises the decision function in isolation, not a corpus of real writes with a real poisoning campaign against it. We recommend, without deciding it here, running the cheap parameterized-test extension before the next revision of this paper; we ship this revision with the honest “next steps” framing rather than delay on it.

06 · ConclusionMemory is what a store does over time

6.1 Where we agree, where we diverge

The diagnosis is correct. Naive contextual agentic memory is a memo. A flat vector store that only appends, never decays, never reconsolidates, and never governs its writes is a working note, and calling its accumulation “learning” is the category error [38] names. We adopt that framing wholesale.

The remedy is one route, not the route. The paper's prescription — a consolidation channel into θ via LoRA/MEMIT/SSR/TTT — is a valid way to obtain the behaviours of memory. But the memo/memory distinction is behavioural, and those behaviours are obtainable in a governed external substrate without touching θ. TerranSoul is the existence proof: consolidation/reconsolidation (C1), decay/tiering (C2), an evolving typed graph (C3), and calibrated metacognition that refits from outcomes (C4) all ship and run with the model frozen; governed self-evolution (C5) is now shipped and unit-tested, though not yet corpus-benched (§5.8). The “Frozen Novice” charge mislabels weight-freezing as expertise-freezing; §5.1 shows behaviour changing across sessions with weights frozen, and §5.2–5.7 show the same frozen substrate competitive across five independent retrieval benchmarks.

Why we hold weights frozen on purpose. Editing θ per session re-imports the very problems an external store solves cleanly: a weight edit is not inspectable, not revertible as a row, not governable at write time, and not portable to another client — and MEMIT-class edits have documented spillover. The properties that make the target paper's security limitation tractable (quarantine, supersession, provenance, audit) are properties of structured data, not of parameters. So the divergence is principled: we accept the compositional ceiling the theorem describes in exchange for an auditable, governable, revertible memory, and route compositional needs to retrieval-time decomposition rather than weight edits.

Delivery reliability is a distinct, under-studied axis. The literature optimises retrieval [1,4,8,9] and reasoning [5,6], and the agentic-RAG survey lists memory management and governance as open [26] — but the variable that dominated our Zork outcome lived in neither place: it was the loop's bookkeeping (turn-count vs. execution-count indexing). Bad delivery propagates as the surveys warn bad writes do [15,31].

The strongest empirical challenge: SIA's two-lever co-evolution. The most direct counter-example to our frozen-weight mandate is SIA [45], which lets a Feedback-Agent rewrite a task agent's scaffold and update its weights, and reports that combining both levers exceeds scaffold-iteration alone on all three of its (off-thesis, §5.9) benchmarks. We concede the force of this on one-shot benchmark maximisation where retraining is permitted. Three things keep the concession bounded:

  • First, it is a different problem. SIA optimises a throwaway, task-specific agent for a single benchmark in an offline, multi-generation loop, keeping no persistent memory between unrelated tasks — no embedder, no typed graph, no retrieval, a look-back of one generation. Scored on the five criteria of §3, SIA's store is a memo, not a memory; its weight path is a training loop, not a memory at all. SIA answers a third, orthogonal question (which lever maximises one benchmark), not the memo-vs-memory question this paper is about — which is exactly why §5.9 excludes its other three benchmarks rather than force a comparison outside the thesis.
  • Second, SIA's own taxonomy partly vindicates us. Its “harness-update school” — improvement with weights frozen — is exactly the lever we claim is real, and its scaffold-only ablation climbing substantially before it plateaus is itself evidence for that claim. The residual disagreement is narrow: whether the fixed-weight lever saturates. SIA shows it can, on a one-shot benchmark; we claim that for a lifelong memory the operative ceiling is the frozen actor's planning depth, not the substrate's (§5.1). Our LawBench engagement (§5.9) is direct evidence the fixed-weight lever need not saturate below weight-editing on this task: on SIA's own headline benchmark our frozen actor + memory scores higher than SIA's weight-trained 120B (76.3–80.0% vs 70.1%).
  • Third, SIA buys its result at the exact price we decline to pay. The opacity, non-revertibility, write-time ungovernability, non-portability, and MEMIT-class spillover catalogued above are acceptable for a disposable champion artifact and disqualifying for an auditable, governed, lifelong self. SIA's objective makes that trade correctly; ours makes the opposite trade correctly. Neither result refutes the other — they optimise different objectives. (Provenance: SIA's weight-update path is paper-reported; an inspection of its public source tree [47] confirmed only the scaffold-editing path, so we treat the weight-editing result as paper-reported, not source-verified.)

What we do take from SIA is methodological: its discipline of moving one lever per generation, to keep each delta attributable, is the right instrument for our own bench loop. Two of the target paper's contributions we intend to adopt: the CGT metric (model-frozen-compatible) and the persistent-injection threat model (MINJA/PoisonedRAG, the explicit adversary BCF-21's poisoning guard must defeat — and now can be tested against with a real corpus-scale bench, §5.8). The weight-editing techniques themselves are not adoptable for the resident actor without violating the frozen-model mandate — a deliberate divergence, not an oversight.

6.2 Limitations and threats to validity

We fold what would otherwise be two separate sections here, since the six-part structure has no dedicated slot for either, and both are load-bearing for a paper whose central claim is measurement honesty.

  • The AGI-pure Zork score is low (10–20) for the 4B. With no taught solution, the effective-4B's multi-step planning ceiling — not the memory layer — binds the score; the expected regime per TALES/BALROG [13,14]. The 12B doubled across episodes (10 → 20) only as a single lucky episode (modal 10 → 10); the self-improvement stack's peak 45 is an across-run accumulation artifact, and the de-confounded per-run result on a fresh task-naïve brain is ~15 (§5.1). The per-episode ceiling remains actor-bound.
  • The 350 is a demonstration, not a discovery. §5.1's delivery-reliability arm gives the brain the solution to isolate delivery reliability; the taught knowledge is demo-only and never enters the AGI-pure substrate.
  • Single ROM, single model family for the acting axis. Zork results are on zork1.z5 with the gemma4 family plus a frontier reference, with qwen2.5:7b the first cross-model point. The full Jericho/TALES surface and broader model families are a deliberate scope boundary.
  • The abstract's earlier “four SIA benchmarks” framing oversold what one benchmark delivers. Stated plainly rather than left as an inline hedge: a prior revision of this paper's framing implied a four-benchmark engagement with SIA. Only LawBench (§5.9) is a clean, on-thesis, apples-to-apples comparison; TriMul is a hardware-normalized estimate, not a measurement; scRNA-seq sits on an incomparable metric scale; MLE-Bench Hard was not run at all. The current framing (§1.5, §5.9) states this explicitly instead of implying parity across four benchmarks that was never measured.
  • Two open number-reconciliation and disclosed-gap items from broadening the evidence base. The LongMemEval-S NDCG@10/MRR headline (§5.3) has two candidate values in this paper's own history (88.8%/89.1% vs. the current 95.1%/95.9%) and we have not completed a full reconciliation pass against every place the older figures were quoted. The 1M-résumé ingest bench (§5.7) discloses a real cross-lingual retrieval gap (en NDCG@10 93.4% vs. vi 45.4%) that a purely lexical channel at this scale does not close.
  • The criteria are arguable. Our five-criterion definition of “memory” is a position, not a theorem; a reader who defines memory as “knowledge in θ” will say we built an excellent database, not a memory. The disagreement is partly terminological.
  • We do not close the compositional gap. Theorem 1 of [38] stands; our multi-hop LoCoMo gap (§5.4) is consistent with it. We claim escape from the memo charge, not from the compositional-ceiling charge, and we do not yet measure CGT.
  • C5 is shipped but not corpus-benched. BCF-21 governance composes shipped guards into a real module (governance.rs) with 21 unit tests, but the persistent-injection regression test is a corpus-scale bench we have identified and not yet run (§5.8) — a narrower gap than “design, not code,” but a real one.
  • The forgetting mechanism is unit-tested, not retrieval-quality-ablated. The decay half-lives (§4.2) have formula-level tests but no corpus-scale recall-with-vs-without-decay comparison (§5.8).
  • Self-advocacy and n. This is a position paper by the system's authors; we mitigate the bias by sourcing every number to a committed file and reporting the figures that cut against us. Several benches are single-trial or n = 2; retrieval numbers across benchmarks are not mutually comparable; several published-framework comparisons (§1.6, §5.2) run each competitor once. None of these is a leaderboard claim.
  • Walkthrough and semantic leakage (Zork specifically). The taught solution is runtime-gated (TAUGHT_SOLUTION_DEMO=1), never written to the memory seed; a grep gate forbids Zork maps/routes/vocabularies in the bridge source (§4.7). The grep gate catches literal strings only; a public-web-pretrained embedder could encode Zork layout — we acknowledge this as an open limitation we have not closed.
  • It does not refute Theorem 1. Conceded explicitly (§5.9, §6.1). The compositional sample-complexity separation is about θ vs. retrieval; our weight-frozen substrate does not contest it on its own ground.

6.3 Memory is what a store does over time

Xu, Dai & Zhang [38] are right that contextual agentic memory, as commonly built, is a memo. We disagree only that the cure must be written into weights. Memory is what a store does over time — consolidate, reconsolidate, forget, structure, calibrate, govern — and a frozen-weight system that does all six is not a memo, whatever its parameters do. TerranSoul ships all five faculties as real code, with four measured directly in a benchmark's outcome and the fifth (governance) unit-tested but not yet corpus-benched, and produces measurable cross-episode behavioural change with its model frozen. The memo charge is therefore escapable without editing θ.

The evidence base is now broader than one game. On the acting axis, externalising task knowledge lifts the unaided frozen 4B from 0 to 10–20 with no task seeds; a frozen 12B doubled across episodes in one run series (10 → 20, n = 1, modal 10 → 10), with the self-improvement stack's peak episode of 45 an across-run accumulation artifact (de-confounded per-run result ~15). With a strategy held fixed, delivery reliability — not added model capacity — moved the same 4B from a non-deterministic 73/177 to a deterministic 350/350, a third agent-performance axis; and the substrate enables distillation through memory: a frontier model solves once, a cheap local model executes thereafter. On the orthogonal retrieval axis, the same substrate beats static-context baselines and sits above or among nine independently-run published memory frameworks on a shared fixture (§5.2), reaches NDCG@10 95.1% on LongMemEval-S (§5.3), scores 63.6 J-score on LoCoMo against a published Mem0 baseline of ~61.7 while disclosing a real multi-hop retrieval gap (§5.4), beats plain-vector RAG frameworks and clarifies (rather than dismisses) GraphRAG's different retrieval shape (§5.5), reaches a 100% needle hit-rate in a retrieval-isolated personal-AI comparison (§5.6), and ingests at 530–2,652 rows/s while openly disclosing a cross-lingual weakness at scale (§5.7). Engaged against SIA on its one on-thesis benchmark, the frozen lever scores higher on LawBench (76.3–80.0% vs 70.1%); its other three benchmarks are explicitly out of scope (§5.9).

The honest scorecard: diagnosis accepted, remedy generalised, two of the interlocutor's ideas (CGT and the injection threat model) adopted, one (weight-editing) declined by mandate — and the central charge answered by construction, now against five retrieval benchmarks and one acting benchmark rather than one. The boundaries are explicit (§6.2): a single ROM and model family for the acting axis, single-trial and n = 2 runs where noted, an H100 kernel figure that is an estimate, an MLE-Bench result not run, two open ablations (ranking-time forgetting, corpus-scale governance) named rather than fabricated, an unresolved LongMemEval-S number reconciliation, a disclosed cross-lingual ingest gap, and a compositional ceiling we do not claim to lift. Together the results are a concrete argument for treating memory as a first-class architectural layer: observable, probeable, governable, and editable in ways a model-internal store is not.
