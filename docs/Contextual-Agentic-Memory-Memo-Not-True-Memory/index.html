Position paper

Contextual Agentic Memory is a Memo, Not True Memory: and a frozen-weight existence proof of the memory the charge demands.

We adopt the title and the indictment of Xu, Dai & Zhang (arXiv:2604.27707) without softening it. Retrieve-and-stuff “agentic memory” is a memo: an ephemeral working note, stateless across the retrieval boundary, with no consolidation, no belief-revision, no forgetting, no metacognition. But we argue the charge lands on naive RAG and scratchpads — not on a governed, consolidating, reconsolidating external substrate. TerranSoul is offered as a working counter-example: true-memory behaviour with the language model’s weights deliberately frozen.

What this paper argues
  • We agree with arXiv:2604.27707’s diagnosis: per-turn context engineering is lookup, not memory, and naming it “learning” is a category error.
  • We contest its dichotomy. The paper splits persistence into context (CC) vs. weights (θ) and prescribes moving knowledge into θ. We argue a third locus exists: a governed, structured, self-evolving external substrate that delivers the behavioural properties of memory (consolidation, reconsolidation, decay, calibration, governance) without editing θ.
  • We propose five falsifiable criteria that separate a memo from memory, and map each to a real, file-referenced TerranSoul faculty.
  • We report only measured numbers, every one sourced to a committed result file, and keep architectural argument explicitly separate from measurement. Limitations are stated plainly: single-PC, n-limited benches, and criteria that are themselves arguable.
Honesty and standpoint. This is a position paper by the TerranSoul project, not a neutral survey, and TerranSoul is the system it advocates. We have a stake in the conclusion; we try to earn it by reporting the numbers that cut against us (the modal Zork I result is 10→10, not the peak 45; the deep cross-episode climb is high-variance and largely an across-run accumulation artifact). We also concede the single most important point to arXiv:2604.27707 up front: TerranSoul does not edit model weights, so on the paper’s own definition our “memory” is weight-frozen by mandate. The argument below is that weight-editing is one route to the properties of memory, not the only one.

AbstractThe memo, the memory, and the frozen-weight middle

Xu, Dai & Zhang argue that the dominant “contextual / agentic memory” paradigm — retrieve passages, inject them into the context window per turn — is not memory but a memo: an ephemeral working note that implements lookup, not learning [1]. They formalise two structurally distinct substrates — context engineering (CC) and weight-based memory (θ) — and prove a sample-complexity separation: retrieval generalises by similarity to stored cases, parametric learning by applying abstract rules to never-seen inputs, and the gap is context-window-independent. Their prescription is a consolidation channel that writes episodic experience into weights.

We accept the diagnosis and reject the implied trichotomy of remedies-of-one. We argue that what makes a store a memory rather than a memo is a set of behavioural properties — offline consolidation and reconsolidation (belief-revision in place), adaptive forgetting, a typed structure that evolves, calibrated metacognition, and governed self-evolution — and that these can be realised in a structured external substrate with model weights frozen. We state five falsifiable criteria, map each to a real TerranSoul faculty with source-file references, and report measured evidence: on LongMemEval-S retrieval (500 questions) the substrate reaches R@5 98.6 % / R@10 99.8 % / R@20 100.0 % at p50 1.0 s and $0 [12]; on the full 1976-query LoCoMo slice, R@10 64.5 % / R@100 85.8 % with documented multi-hop gaps [12]; on ZorkGPT the externalised memory lifts a frozen 4B from 0 to 10–20 and a frozen 12B’s per-run cross-episode score to ~15 (peak 45 via across-run accumulation, honestly de-confounded) with weights never touched [12]. We are explicit about what this does not show: it does not refute the paper’s compositional-generalisation theorem, which is about θ vs. retrieval and which our weight-frozen design does not contest on its own terms. It shows, rather, that the “memo” charge is escapable without θ-editing — that consolidation, decay, structure, calibration and governance are the operative distinction, and that a frozen-weight system can have all five.

01 · IntroductionA memo is not a memory

A memo is a note you write to get through the afternoon: you jot what you need, you act on it, and you throw it away. Nothing about today’s note changes how you write tomorrow’s. Xu, Dai & Zhang argue that this is precisely what “agentic memory” has become — a per-turn working note assembled by retrieval, used, and discarded across the retrieval boundary [1]. The system retrieves the k nearest passages, pastes them into the prompt, generates, and the only thing that persists is more undigested passages in the same flat store. They call treating this as learning a “category error with provable consequences,” and we think they are right.

Their constructive claim is sharper than the critique. Knowledge persistence, they argue, has two structurally distinct substrates. Context engineering (CC) injects information into the window via RAG, vector stores, or scratchpads; it can generalise only by similarity to stored cases. Weight-based memory (θ) writes knowledge into parameters via fine-tuning; it generalises by applying abstract rules to inputs never seen before. Their Theorem 1 (a compositional sample-complexity separation, via Fano’s inequality and VC bounds) states that retrieval needs Ω(k2) stored examples to hit a target accuracy on k concept pairs, while parametric learning needs only O(d + log(1/δ)/δ) — and the gap is context-window-independent. Bigger windows do not close it. From this they derive four structural limitations — definitional (lookup cannot extrapolate), structural (a provable generalisation ceiling), dynamic (the “Frozen Novice”: agents accumulate notes but never develop expertise because weights never change between sessions), and security (injected malicious content becomes persistent across all future sessions) — and prescribe a consolidation channel: an asynchronous background pathway that distils episodic experience into weights (LoRA, MEMIT, SSR, TTT, self-distillation).

We agree with the diagnosis and with most of the constructive frame. Our disagreement is narrow and, we think, important. The paper equates “true memory” with “knowledge in θ.” We argue that memory is better individuated by what it does than by where it lives: a substrate is a memory, not a memo, when it consolidates offline, revises beliefs in place, forgets adaptively, grows a typed structure, calibrates its own confidence, and governs its own evolution. Those are behavioural commitments, and — this is the wager of the paper — they are realisable in a structured external store while the language model’s weights stay frozen. We are not claiming this closes the paper’s compositional gap (it does not; §6); we are claiming the memo-vs-memory distinction is not the same distinction as CC-vs-θ, and that conflating them concedes too much to fine-tuning.

This matters operationally. TerranSoul runs under a standing mandate to keep its local model (gemma4:12b-it-qat) frozen — an explicit project constraint, not an oversight. If “memory” required θ-editing, TerranSoul could have none. The rest of this paper asks whether that is true, states criteria that would settle it, and maps each criterion to a shipped faculty with file references and measured behaviour.

02 · Related workThe 2026 agentic-memory debate

The target paper. Xu, Dai & Zhang [1] is the proximate interlocutor. Its empirical scaffolding (which we report as their evidence, not independently verified here) cites ParamMem-style results that parametric storage outperforms retrieval with the gap widening on novel tasks; Ovadia 2024 that RAG does not improve compositional reasoning; SCAN/COGS multi-hop results where fine-tuning beats RAG; and two memory-injection attacks — MINJA at 98.2 % success and PoisonedRAG at 90 % with five adversarial texts — as evidence that a persistent store is a persistent attack surface. It explicitly names MemGPT (context paging without weight updates), Reflexion (“verbal RL” leaving weights frozen), and Voyager (skills stored as retrievable code) as “CC-engineering only.” For benchmark designers it proposes a Compositional Generalization over Time (CGT) metric: does accuracy on novel concept combinations rise with experience, not just recall@k?

The dual-process line. DCPM — “Memory Beyond Recall: a Dual-Process Cognitive Memory System” [2] — splits a System-1 “daytime writer” (raw episodic capture) from a System-2 “nighttime” schema-induction pass that distils reusable strategies and treats failures as first-class memory. Its raw-recall-vs-distilled-strategy split is exactly the memo/memory line drawn behaviourally rather than by substrate, and it validates the consolidation/reflection architecture we describe in §4 without ever editing weights.

The governance line. SSGM — the Stability and Safety Governed Memory framework [3] — frames drift, poisoning, and unbounded growth as the missing governance layer over an evolving store. Its key observation cuts directly at the target paper’s security limitation: the danger only exists once memory truly persists and self-evolves; a scratchpad has nothing to govern. Governance, on this view, is the price of real memory — and its presence is evidence that a store has crossed from memo to memory. SSGM is the stated basis for TerranSoul’s governed-self-evolution faculty (§4.5).

The surveys and taxonomies. A cluster of 2026 surveys converges on a shared picture: “memory” is the externalisation layer (memory + skills + protocols + harness) around a frozen LLM, distinct from per-turn context/retrieval. These include “Externalization in LLM Agents — a Unified Review” [4], “Towards Autonomous Memory Agents” (a learned, Thompson-sampling retrieval policy) [5], “Are We Ready For An Agent-Native Memory System?” (which argues conservative consolidation beats aggressive reorganisation) [6], and DeepMind’s 10-faculty cognitive framework [7]. Three further taxonomy surveys (arXiv:2602.06052, 2602.19320, 2603.07670) are frequently cited in this space; we record them as cited rather than independently verified against their abstracts here, and flag that caveat explicitly.

The production systems the target paper indicts. Mem0 [8] is a production memory layer that distinguishes extracted/consolidated memory from raw conversation context and reports a LoCoMo QA J-score of 68.5 %; in the paper’s terms it is still CC-only (no weight pathway). Letta / MemGPT [9] is memory-as-OS context paging (LoCoMo QA J-score 83.2 % [11]) and is the canonical “memo” the paper names. Zep / Graphiti [10] is a temporal knowledge-graph memory (LoCoMo QA J-score 34.53 % [11]) — closest in shape to TerranSoul’s typed edge store, but with no decay/reconsolidation/calibration loop layered over it. We stress an honesty point inherited from our results matrix: the Mem0/Letta/Zep figures are end-to-end QA accuracy (J-score), not retrieval recall, and are not comparable to our retrieval numbers in §5 [11].

03 · The distinctionFive criteria that separate a memo from a memory

To make the debate falsifiable we propose five criteria. They are behavioural: they ask what a store does over time, not where its bits sit. A pure retrieval-and-stuff pipeline fails all five; the target paper’s θ-editing remedy passes some by construction. We claim a structured external substrate can pass all five with weights frozen, and we concede in §6 that the criteria are themselves arguable.

#CriterionMemo (retrieve-and-stuff)Memory (the demand)
C1Offline consolidation & reconsolidationnone; the store only grows by appendidle replay distils experience; reactivation makes a belief labile and revises it in place
C2Adaptive forgetting / decaynothing decays; old notes rank forevertime-anchored decay and tiered eviction; salience, not recency-of-write, survives
C3Typed structure that evolvesflat list of passagestyped, directional graph with supersedes/contradicts edges that self-correct
C4Calibrated metacognitionno model of its own reliabilitya second-order monitor that estimates calibrated confidence and recalibrates from outcomes
C5Governed self-evolutionany write persists; injection is permanentautonomous writes pass a drift/poisoning/growth gate; uncorroborated writes are quarantined

Two of the target paper’s four limitations are about these criteria. The “Frozen Novice” (their dynamic limitation) is the absence of C1: a store that only appends never develops expertise. The security limitation (“evil²” persistent injection) is the absence of C5: a store with no write-time governance carries an attacker’s payload into every future session. We take these two seriously and answer them directly in §4.1 and §4.5. The remaining two limitations — definitional and structural — are genuinely about CC-vs-θ and the compositional ceiling; we do not claim the five criteria dissolve them, and we say so in §6.

04 · Existence proofTerranSoul, mapped criterion-by-criterion

TerranSoul is a local-first companion (Vue + Tauri + Rust + SQLite + Ollama) whose “brain” is a Rust memory server reached over the Model Context Protocol. By project mandate the language model’s weights are frozen; all learning is externalised into the substrate. Below, each criterion of §3 is mapped to a real faculty with source-file references. We label each honestly: what it does, and what it does not (in particular, every faculty strengthens or revises stored structure, never θ).

4.1  C1 — Consolidation and reconsolidation (the answer to the Frozen Novice)

The reconsolidation module (src-tauri/src/memory/reconsolidation.rs) implements an idle complementary-learning-systems (CLS) replay pass. consolidate() / consolidate_conn() (lines 423 / 428) sample the highest-salience episodic rows that are due under FSRS-style spacing — salience() (line 207) weighs importance, access count and age; next_stability() (line 223) schedules the next review — and strengthen_memory() (line 347) raises a row’s Laplace-smoothed confidence, increments its success count, and stamps a consolidation_run_id. This is the hippocampus→neocortex replay the target paper says agents lack: experience is distilled offline into strengthened generalisations rather than left as raw episodics.

The harder property is reconsolidation: when a belief is reactivated it becomes labile and can be revised in place. reactivate() (line 452) marks a row labile; should_reconsolidate() (line 256) gates whether a new, similar observation should overwrite rather than co-exist; and reconsolidate_in_place() / reconsolidate_in_place_conn() (lines 476–519) perform belief-revision-in-place — correcting a stale belief cleanly instead of stacking a contradicting duplicate beside it. A memo cannot do this: appending the correction leaves the error retrievable forever. Honest caveat: this revises stored confidence and content, not θ. The Frozen Novice charge is that weights never change between sessions; our answer is that behaviour changes between sessions because the substrate the frozen model reads has changed — which §5 shows is measurable.

4.2  C2 — Three tiers and adaptive forgetting

The store (src-tauri/src/memory/store.rs) defines a MemoryTier enum (line 1298): Short (evicted at session end), Working (held until consolidation), and Long (permanent, vector-indexed). Forgetting is not incidental — it is a typed, time-anchored function. src-tauri/src/memory/confidence_decay.rs applies a per-cognitive-kind multiplicative decay confidence × 0.5^(age/half_life) with half-lives of 365 days for judgments, 90 for semantic, 60 for procedural, 7 for episodic (lines 7–10, 29–32), folded into the retrieval RRF score. A correction (judgment) is durable for a year; a time-stamped episode loses rank in a week. This is the inverse of a memo: salience, not recency-of-write, determines what survives, and low-salience material ages out and is pruned rather than ranking forever.

4.3  C3 — A typed knowledge graph that evolves

The edge store (src-tauri/src/memory/edges.rs) holds typed, directional edges. COMMON_RELATION_TYPES (line 32) includes supersedes, contradicts, derived_from, governs, and depends_on; the (src_id, dst_id, rel_type) tuple is unique so extraction is idempotent, and an EdgeSource records provenance (user / LLM / auto). add_edge() (line 226) and add_edges_batch() (line 249) are exposed to any client over MCP via brain_add_edge (src-tauri/src/ai_integrations/mcp/tools.rs:1120). This buys two things a flat note list cannot: multi-hop recall (walk from a vector hit to keyword-disjoint memories) and a structure that self-corrects — a supersedes or contradicts edge demotes a stale claim rather than leaving two equal-rank contradictions in the store. The structure evolves; the memo’s does not.

4.4  C4 — Calibrated metacognition

The metacognition module (src-tauri/src/memory/metacognition.rs) is a second-order monitor that estimates a calibrated confidence in the agent’s own answer before it is emitted — the “know-when-you-don’t-know” faculty. route() (line 361) turns the score into one of answer / deliberate / seek-more / abstain. Crucially, the calibration is learned from outcomes: recalibrate() (line 559) refits a Platt map from the logged calibration data against realised five-class session outcomes (minimising log-loss / ECE) and rewrites the brain-seeded config rows. The curve is not hardcoded; it improves with experience. This is the metacognitive faculty the “Frozen Novice” is said to lack — and it improves with weights frozen, because the thing being refit is a config row in the substrate, not a parameter in θ.

4.5  C5 — Governed self-evolution (the answer to persistent injection)

The target paper’s sharpest practical point is security: MINJA-style injection at 98.2 % and PoisonedRAG at 90 % with five texts mean that a store which accepts any write carries an attacker’s payload into every future session. TerranSoul’s answer is a write-time governance gate, BCF-21 (docs/brain-advanced-design.md:7649–7690). Honest status caveat: BCF-21 is a DESIGN, not yet a shipped .rs module. The design routes every autonomous (brain-authored) durable write through brain_govern_memory, composing existing structural signals: a drift guard (reusing the C1 reconsolidation/corroboration path), a poisoning/injection guard (source-concentration and source-trust provenance), a growth guard (the decay/prune budget of C2), and quarantine of peer-brain imports until corroborated. User writes bypass the gate; only the brain’s own writes are governed. It cites SSGM [3] as its basis. The point for this paper is that governance is possible and meaningful only because the store is real memory — a memo has nothing to govern — and that the persistent-injection threat is the explicit adversary this gate must defeat.

4.6  The closed loop: write → manage → read

The five faculties compose into a loop. Write: a policy (src-tauri/src/memory/auto_learn.rs) decides when to extract memories from a session (default every ~10 turns with a cooldown). Manage / self-tune: idle passes run consolidation (§4.1) and recalibration (§4.4), strengthening salient rows and refitting the confidence map. Read: hybrid FTS5 + vector + KG retrieval with rerank. Every turn writes; idle passes manage and self-tune the brain’s own seeded weights; retrieval reads. The learning lives in the substrate, not in θ — which is exactly the architecture the target paper says is impossible to call “memory.” The remainder of this paper asks whether the loop produces the behavioural signatures of memory, measurably.

05 · EvidenceWhat is measured, and what it does and does not show

Every number below is from a committed result file in the project’s benchmark matrix [11,12]. We separate measurement from argument: these results establish that the substrate is a competitive retriever and that it produces cross-episode behavioural change with weights frozen. They do not measure compositional generalisation over time (the target paper’s CGT metric, which we have not yet implemented), and they do not refute its θ-vs-retrieval theorem.

5.1 Retrieval quality (the orthogonal axis)

Per-type R@5 is uniformly high (single-session-assistant 100.0 %, knowledge-update 98.7 %, temporal-reasoning 98.5 %) [12]. On the harder full 1976-query MTEB LoCoMo slice (BENCH-LCM-1, re-measured 2026-06-28), the EmbeddingGemma vector path (rrf_emb) scores R@10 64.5 % / R@100 85.8 % / NDCG@10 49.9 % / MRR@100 48.3 %, with the lexical rrf at R@10 57.2 % / R@100 76.9 % [12]. Temporal reasoning is strong (R@10 ~90 %); multi-hop and open-domain are documented gaps requiring query decomposition / iterative retrieval — an honest weakness, and (notably) exactly the compositional region the target paper predicts retrieval will struggle with.

Token efficiency (regenerated 2026-06-25, post RRF-fix): hybrid_search_rrf no-vector reaches R@10 66.8 % / NDCG@10 95.0 % at 2,748 retrieved tokens/query vs. a keyword-only quality leader at 67.1 % / 8,245 tokens — ~3× fewer tokens at near-equal quality [11].

An instructive contrast with frontier agentic retrieval. Run as an agentic retriever (grep/read → ranked top-20, cloud claude-haiku-4-5), Claude Code + GENesis-AGI scores R@5 43.6 % / R@10 57.2 % / R@20 72.3 % / NDCG@10 83.9 % / MRR 100.0 % at ~53 s/query and ~$6, vs. the local hybrid substrate’s 98.6 % R@5 at 1.0 s p50 and $0 on the same retrieval shape [11]. Frontier grep-and-read lands only in the vector-cluster recall range (with perfect first-hit ranking) at two orders of magnitude more latency and non-local cost. This is a measurement about retrieval modalities, not about the memo/memory thesis; we report it because it bears on whether “the agent will just search” is a substitute for a structured store. It is not, on these numbers.

5.2 Behavioural change with weights frozen (the Frozen-Novice rebuttal)

The most direct evidence against the “Frozen Novice” claim is the ZorkGPT long-horizon bench, where the language model’s weights are provably untouched and only the external substrate varies [11,12]. AGI-pure (no game-specific seeds), the substrate lifts the same frozen 4B (gemma4:e4b) from 0 (both the bare-LLM and ZorkGPT-default controls) to 10–20, and removes its fixation loops; cross-episode behavioural change is verified (a new room, Up a Tree, reached in a later episode via a reflection hydrated from an earlier one), at 0/1682 MCP errors. On a frozen 12B (gemma4:12b-it-qat) self-improvement campaign, the de-confounded per-run cross-episode score is ~15 on a fresh task-naive brain, with a peak single episode of 45 reached via across-run accumulation on a persistent brain — a one-time lucky discovery carried forward, not repeatable single-run self-improvement. We label this distinction explicitly because it is the difference between an honest result and an inflated one.

The argument this supports is narrow and we state it narrowly: behaviour changed across sessions with the model’s weights frozen, driven entirely by changes in the external substrate. That is a counter-example to “agents accumulate notes but never develop expertise because weights never change.” Expertise here is operational (avoiding a fatal move learned last episode; reaching a room via a hydrated reflection), and it is bounded — the score ceiling is set by the frozen actor’s planning depth, not by the memory layer, which is itself on-thesis: the substrate keeps unblocking progress and the residual bound is the model’s, not the memory’s.

5.3 What this evidence does not show

  • It does not refute Theorem 1. The compositional sample-complexity separation is a statement about θ vs. retrieval generalisation. Our weight-frozen substrate does not contest it on its own ground; our LoCoMo multi-hop gap is, if anything, consistent with it.
  • It does not measure CGT. We do not yet have a compositional-generalisation-over-time metric. Adopting one (§6) is the cleanest way to put our cross-episode behavioural-change claim on a principled footing — and to test it adversarially.
  • The benchmarks are n-limited and single-PC. The Zork cross-game result is n = 2; the deep cross-episode climb is high-variance; retrieval numbers are cross-benchmark-incomparable by construction [11]. None of these is a leaderboard claim.

06 · DiscussionWhere we agree, where we diverge

The diagnosis is correct. Naive contextual agentic memory is a memo. A flat vector store that only appends, never decays, never reconsolidates, and never governs its writes is a working note, and calling its accumulation “learning” is the category error the paper names. We adopt that framing wholesale; it is the title of this paper.

The remedy is one route, not the route. The paper’s prescription — a consolidation channel into θ via LoRA / MEMIT / SSR / TTT — is a valid way to obtain the behaviours of memory. But the memo/memory distinction is behavioural, and those behaviours are obtainable in a governed external substrate without touching θ. TerranSoul is the existence proof: consolidation and reconsolidation (C1), decay and tiering (C2), an evolving typed graph (C3), and calibrated metacognition that refits from outcomes (C4) all ship and run with the model frozen; governed self-evolution (C5) is designed and partially composed from shipped guards. The “Frozen Novice” charge mislabels weight-freezing as expertise-freezing; §5.2 shows behaviour changing across sessions with weights frozen.

Why we hold weights frozen on purpose. This is not merely a constraint we tolerate. Editing θ per session re-imports the very problems an external store solves cleanly: a weight edit is not inspectable, not revertible as a row, not governable at write time, and not portable to another client — and MEMIT-class edits have documented spillover. The properties that make the target paper’s security limitation tractable (quarantine, supersession, provenance, audit) are properties of structured data, not of parameters. So the divergence is principled: we accept the compositional ceiling the theorem describes in exchange for an auditable, governable, revertible memory — and we route compositional needs to retrieval-time decomposition rather than weight edits. Where a frontier model has already solved a task, we capture its solution into the substrate once and let a cheap frozen model replay it (the “distillation through memory” result of the companion paper) — distillation into data, not weights.

Adopting what the paper gets right. Two of its contributions we intend to take up. (1) The CGT metric is the right instrument for our central claim and is model-frozen-compatible; adding a “compositional generalisation over time” axis to our LongMemEval / LoCoMo / ZorkGPT adapters would test whether accuracy on novel concept combinations rises with experience, not just static recall@k. (2) The persistent-injection threat model (MINJA / PoisonedRAG) is a sharper framing than our current design text and should be folded in as the explicit adversary BCF-21’s poisoning guard must defeat, with a regression test that an uncorroborated poisoned peer/connector write cannot reach the durable store. We are explicit that the paper’s weight-editing techniques themselves (LoRA/MEMIT/SSR/TTT-into-θ) are not adoptable for the resident actor without violating the frozen-model mandate; that is a deliberate architectural divergence, not an oversight, and at most they could ever be a separate offline opt-in adapter distilled from traces.

07 · LimitationsStated plainly

  • The criteria are arguable. Our five-criterion definition of “memory” is a position, not a theorem. A reader who defines memory as “knowledge in θ” (the target paper’s definition) will say we have built an excellent database, not a memory. We think the behavioural definition is more useful, but it is contestable, and the disagreement is partly terminological.
  • We do not close the compositional gap. Theorem 1 stands; our multi-hop LoCoMo gap is consistent with it. We claim escape from the memo charge, not from the compositional-ceiling charge.
  • Single-PC, n-limited evidence. All results are single-machine, local-model, and several are single-trial or n = 2 (cross-game Zork). The headline Zork peak (45) is an across-run accumulation artifact, not repeatable per-run self-improvement; the modal AGI-pure Zork I result is 10→10. Retrieval numbers across benchmarks are not mutually comparable.
  • C5 is partly design, not code. BCF-21 governed self-evolution is a design composing shipped guards (reconsolidation, source-trust, decay budget); it is not yet a single shipped module, and the persistent-injection regression test is proposed, not yet written.
  • Self-advocacy. This is a position paper by the system’s authors. We have mitigated, not eliminated, the bias by sourcing every number to a committed file and reporting the figures that cut against us; a reader should still weight it as advocacy.

08 · ConclusionThe memo charge is escapable without editing θ

Xu, Dai & Zhang are right that contextual agentic memory, as commonly built, is a memo. We disagree only that the cure must be written into weights. Memory is what a store does over time — consolidate, reconsolidate, forget, structure, calibrate, govern — and a frozen-weight system that does all six is not a memo, whatever its parameters do. TerranSoul ships the first five faculties and designs the sixth, and produces measurable cross-episode behavioural change with its model frozen. The compositional ceiling the paper proves is real and we do not claim to have lifted it; we claim that the memo-vs-memory distinction and the CC-vs-θ distinction are not the same distinction, and that conflating them concedes to fine-tuning a victory it has not earned. The honest scorecard: diagnosis accepted, remedy generalised, two of the paper’s ideas (CGT and the injection threat model) adopted, one (weight-editing) declined by mandate — and the central charge answered by construction.
